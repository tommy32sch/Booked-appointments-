Soft tissue part 2 — key

Ugly injuries steal your eyes. Ask: will this kill them in the next minute? If no, note it and keep going. Find a major bleed → stop and treat. What actually kills soft-tissue patients: bleeding (hemorrhagic shock) first, and later infection (septic shock). The wound name is rarely the life threat. The amount of blood is.

Skin layers: epidermis = outer layer. Dermis = inner layer (vessels, nerves, sweat glands). Skin jobs: keep germs out, feel the world, control body heat, control fluid. After a big burn stops, worry about them getting cold (hypothermia). Burned skin cannot hold heat. Burn depth: superficial = 1st degree (epidermis only). Partial thickness = 2nd degree (into the dermis). Full thickness = 3rd degree (through the skin). Full burn talk comes later.

Open = skin broken or punctured. Closed = skin intact. Infection risk jumps when the skin is open. Open fracture = broken bone + a cut in the nearby skin. The bone does not have to stick out.

After a cut: the vessel clamps down so blood slows and a clot can form. If the hole is too big, you add pressure to help that clamp. After the bleed stops, vessels open up (inflammation) to bring oxygen, food, and white cells. The body cannot send extra blood until the bleed is stopped. Extra blood too soon = they bleed out.

Contusion / bruise = bleeding under the skin; skin is not cut. Ecchymosis = the color of a bruise (blue/purple → green/yellow). Hematoma = a bigger pool of blood in tissue or a body space. Face and scalp swell a lot because they have many vessels. A bruise is almost never a 5-second killer by itself.

Crush injury = heavy force on the body. How bad = how hard × how long. Crush syndrome = crush lasting 4+ hours. Dead cells dump potassium (K+) and junk. When they lift the weight, K+ floods the body. Two killers: heart stops and kidneys fail (rhabdomyolysis / myoglobin clogs kidneys). Tourniquet the limb BEFORE they lift. Do not loosen a tourniquet unless a doctor says so. A bleed tourniquet left on 4+ hours can CREATE this problem.

Compartment syndrome = squeeze from the inside (“internal tourniquet”). Hurt tissue swells until skin crushes vessels and nerves. Takes hours — book about 6 to 12 hours — not minutes. A bit more common in kids.

Open wound types: abrasion = friction scrape. Laceration = jagged cut. Incision = smooth cut. Avulsion = tissue torn into a flap or ripped clean off (a chunk). Amputation = a body part is off. Puncture = force on a small spot (little hole outside, bad bleed inside possible).

Avulsion care: if still a flap, lay it back where it belongs and bandage. Do not wash dirt out of the hole — dirt may be plugging the bleed. Surface dirt you can brush off.

Amputation care: still try direct pressure first if it might work (finger, small child arm). Big adult arm or leg → go straight to a tourniquet. Bring the part if you can: wrap in sterile dressing → put in plastic → keep cool. Do not delay the ambulance hunting for the part. Life over limb.

Chest and upper-belly holes: use plastic (occlusive / chest seal). Put one from the sternal notch down to the belly button, front and back. A high belly hole can still be a chest hole. Bullet in and out: seal both holes. Tape 3 sides so the dressing can burp (tension pneumothorax risk).

RICE (rest, ice, compression, elevation) is fine for an isolated sprained ankle. Critical multi-trauma patients get shock care and go.

Every bandage does at least one of three jobs: stop more injury, stop more infection, stop bleeding. Moist list is short: anything that should be inside and is now outside (guts, eyes). Chest holes get plastic. Most other wounds get dry sterile. Do not push organs or a dislocated joint back in.

Practice

Do this: look at an ugly injury and ask if it kills in the next minute. You did it right if a non-killer got noted and you kept going, and a major bleed got treated now.

Do this: name epidermis then dermis, and say big-burn patients can get hypothermic. You did it right if superficial/1st, partial/2nd, and full/3rd lined up.

Do this: define open fracture as break + nearby skin cut. You did it right if bone sticking out was not required.

Do this: say vessel clamp → clot, then inflammation only after the bleed stops. You did it right if extra blood before the bleed stopped was refused.

Do this: match contusion = bruise, ecchymosis = color, hematoma = bigger pool. You did it right if face/scalp swell a lot.

Do this: crush syndrome = 4+ hours; TQ the limb BEFORE they lift; do not loosen unless a doctor says. You did it right if K+ flood, heart stop, and kidney fail were the killers.

Do this: call compartment an internal tourniquet that takes hours (about 6–12), not minutes. You did it right if kids were noted as a bit more common.

Do this: name laceration jagged and incision smooth; avulsion = tissue chunk; amputation = body part. You did it right if abrasion was a friction scrape and puncture was a small-spot hole.

Do this: lay an avulsion flap back and bandage; brush surface dirt only. You did it right if you did not wash dirt from the hole.

Do this: big adult limb amputation → TQ; wrap part sterile → plastic → cool; do not delay transport. You did it right if finger/small-child-arm still tried pressure first.
