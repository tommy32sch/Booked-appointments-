# Chest injuries — detailed

Chest holds airway, breathing, and circulation. Surgery is the real fix. Platinum 10 minutes. XABCs first. Board later.

## Big rules

Hole in the chest wall: plastic dressing (occlusive / chest seal / 3-sided). Gauze lets air through. Seal with plastic: occlusive dressing, chest seal, or “non-porous dressing.” Tape 3 sides or use a vented commercial seal so extra air can burp out. If it clogs: peel it, let it vent, reseal. In-and-out wound: seal both holes. Occlusive on any hole from the sternal notch to the belly button, front and back. “Convert open to closed” = seal it.

Tension pneumo vs hemothorax look the same except the neck veins. Fat veins = air. Flat veins = bled out.

Tension vs tamponade: both shock + JVD. Tamponade has two good lungs and a narrow pulse pressure.

Flail chest: do not tape a bulky dressing on it. Do not use sandbags. That is the old answer.

Irregular pulse after a steering-wheel hit = bruised heart. Kid drops after a ball to the chest = commotio cordis. CPR + AED.

## Anatomy

Box = ribs + spine + diaphragm. Diaphragm drops on inhale (sucks air in). Rises on exhale (air falls out).

Upper-belly stab still gets a chest seal. You do not know where the diaphragm was.

Adults breathe with diaphragm + intercostals. Kids are almost all diaphragm.

Under each rib: nerve, vein, artery. Broken ribs hurt a lot, worse when they breathe, and can bleed.

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Parietal pleura | Lining of the chest wall |
| Visceral pleura | Lining on the lung |
| Pneumothorax | Air in that gap |
| Hemothorax | Blood in that gap |
| Mediastinum | Middle space: heart, aorta, vena cava, trachea, esophagus |

Phrenic nerve = C3, C4, C5. Keeps the diaphragm alive. Cord cut above C5 → they do not breathe. Below C5 → diaphragm still works, intercostals do not. Adults fatigue. Kids already live on the diaphragm, so breathing holds up a bit better.

## Breathing words

Ventilation: air in and out. Rate, rhythm, quality. What you count.

Respiration: gas exchange in the alveoli.

Oxygenation: oxygen onto red cells. Then circulation delivers it.

They can breathe 20 times a minute with good rise and still be blue. Ventilation is fine. Gas exchange is not. Crackles = fluid in the alveoli (water or blood).

The body sucks air in (negative pressure). A BVM pushes air in (positive pressure), so some air also goes to the stomach.

## What you will see

Open = hole in the skin. Closed = skin intact. Bruises may not show yet.

Damage is instant. Symptoms can keep getting worse as the lung leaks.

Crepitus = broken-rib grind.

Hemoptysis = coughing blood = lung. Hematemesis = vomiting blood = gut.

Pink frothy sputum = pulmonary edema. Not the same as frank blood.

One-sided chest rise or quiet lung = that lung is not filling.

Paradoxical motion = one segment moves the wrong way. Flail. Not the whole side.

Default shock in trauma is bleed. Then think obstructive: tension pneumo or tamponade.

## Pneumothorax

Air around the lung squeezes it down from the outside. It does not pop empty like a balloon.

| Stage | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Simple | Air leaked. Heart is not squeezed yet. Small hole may seal. Big hole goes to tension faster. Closed cause is often a broken rib or clavicle. |
| Open / sucking | Hole in the chest wall. Negative pressure sucks air in the wrong door. |
| Tension | Too much air. Pushes the heart and the other lung. Obstructive shock. |

Open chest hole: seal with plastic as above. Paramedic needle is for a CLOSED tension pneumo (no hole to vent). We do not needle.

Tension signs:

- Shock: low BP, fast heart, fast breathing, cool pale clammy.
- JVD — blood cannot get into the squeezed heart.
- Quiet or absent breath sounds on the bad side. One-sided chest rise.
- Trachea pushed AWAY from the bad side. Only used for tension pneumo in this chapter.

## Hemothorax vs tension

Both: chest trauma, shock, quiet on one side.

| Clue | Think |
| --- | --- |
| Neck veins fat / JVD | Tension pneumo. Blood is backed up. |
| Neck veins flat / collapsed | Hemothorax. They bled into the chest. No blood left in the veins. |

Crackles are the wrong clue. Hemothorax blood is outside the lung. Crackles mean fluid inside the alveoli.

Hemothorax is a bleed. They need surgery and blood. Go.

## Cardiac tamponade

The sac around the heart fills (usually blood after trauma). The sac only stretches so far, then it squeezes the heart. The heart muscle itself is OK. Obstructive shock.

Beck’s triad:

- JVD
- Muffled / quiet heart sounds
- Narrow pulse pressure (systolic and diastolic squeeze together). Opposite of Cushing’s wide pulse pressure.

Tension vs tamponade: both have chest trauma, shock, and JVD. Tamponade does not have a quiet lung or a shifted trachea. Two good lungs → think heart, not lung.

We do not drain it. Old paramedic needle into the sac is gone.

## Flail chest

Several ribs broken in more than one place. That segment floats.

On inhale the rest of the chest goes out and the flail sinks in. On exhale it does the opposite.

Do not tape a bulky dressing over it. Do not use sandbags. That is the old answer. It makes breathing worse.

ABCs, oxygen, shock care, go. Watch for a bruised lung underneath.

## Bruised lung and bruised heart

| Injury | What to know |
| --- | --- |
| Pulmonary contusion | Blood in the alveoli. Crackles. Can take hours. May kill days later. No CPAP in trauma (can blow a hole in a damaged lung; shock is also a no). BVM only if they are not breathing well. |
| Cardiac / myocardial contusion | Steering wheel to the chest + shock + irregular pulse. Bruised heart does not pump well (cardiogenic shock). Electricity does not travel well through bruise. Heart cannot rest. |
| Sternal fracture | Not the bone. The heart is two inches behind it. |
| Clavicle fracture | Easy to break. Can poke the top of the lung. Arm held forward across the chest. |

## Traumatic asphyxia

A huge crush on the chest shoves blood up into the head.

- Purple / bruised face and neck.
- Bloodshot whites of the eyes.
- JVD.

ABCs and go. Vessels in the brain may have popped too.

## Commotio cordis

A blow to the chest at the exact millisecond the heart is recharging. Electricity fires and recharges at once. They drop in V-fib.

Classic: kid hit by a baseball. Also Damar Hamlin.

Heart was fine one second before. CPR + AED works if you start fast.

Usually diagnosed after they rule everything else out. On scene: arrest after a chest blow → treat the arrest.

## Care clues

XABCs first. Board later. Platinum 10. Surgery is the answer.

C3-4-5 keeps the diaphragm alive.

Coughing blood = lung. Vomiting blood = gut.

Fat neck veins + quiet one lung = tension. Flat neck veins + quiet one lung = hemo.

Fat neck veins + two good lungs + narrow pulse pressure = tamponade.

Flail: do not bulky-dress.

Irregular pulse after blunt chest = cardiac contusion.

Kid drops after a ball to the chest = CPR and AED.

## Add-on: EKG timing, pulse pressure, aorta

Commotio cordis — when on the EKG:

Normal beat: P wave (atria fire) → QRS (ventricles fire) → T wave (heart recharges).

The hit that drops them is during the T wave — the recharge / repolarization phase.

Force the heart to fire again while it is still recharging → V-fib.

Heart was fine the beat before. CPR + AED if they drop.

Pulse pressure recap:

- Head / Cushing / ICP: high and WIDE. Systolic flies up. Example 200/90.
- Cardiac tamponade: NARROW and low. The two numbers squeeze together.

You cannot pinch the aorta:

A big artery in an arm or leg: tourniquet or pinch. You only shut off that limb.

Aorta in the chest: if you pinch it, nothing gets out to the body. We have no access unless the chest is already wide open.

Those bleeds need the hospital / surgery.

Aorta extras (not EMT treatment):

- Torn aorta from trauma is often dead on scene.
- Medical aneurysm that has not burst: tearing chest pain.
- Hospital may put a mesh sleeve / sock around the aorta to keep it from ballooning.
- That is not the same as a bypass graft (graft = take a vessel from somewhere else).
