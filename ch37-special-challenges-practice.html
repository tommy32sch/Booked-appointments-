Special challenges — practice

22 type-in facts. Tap to reveal.

Tap a card to reveal the answer.

1. On a special-challenges call, what often has nothing to do with the complaint?
Answer
the Down syndrome or the vent
Why
What did we get called for? The Down syndrome or the vent may have nothing to do with it.
2. Not talking — what two possibilities do you check?
Answer
may be normal for them, or new and bad
Why
Find their baseline. Not talking may be normal for them. Or it may be new and bad.
3. Blind patient — what do you do before you touch them?
Answer
say everything before you touch them
Why
Blind: say everything before you touch them.
4. Whistling hearing aid — what does it mean?
Answer
not seated or a dead battery
Why
Whistling aid = not seated or a dead battery.
5. Critical patient at home with a service animal — what do you do with the dog?
Answer
leave and arrange later
Why
Service animal: critical at home → leave and arrange later.
6. Down syndrome airway — why is it harder, and what may be easier?
Answer
bigger tongue; jaw thrust may be easier
Why
Bigger tongue. Harder airway. Jaw thrust may be easier than a big head-tilt.
7. About what percent of Down syndrome patients have unstable C1–C2?
Answer
about 15%
Why
About 15% have an unstable C1–C2 joint (atlanto-axial).
8. VP shunt drains where?
Answer
brain ventricles → belly
Why
VP shunt: brain ventricles → belly.
9. VA shunt drains where?
Answer
brain ventricles → heart
Why
VA shunt: brain ventricles → heart.
10. Shunt clogs or infects — what vital pattern do you get?
Answer
headache, vomit, slow heart, high wide BP, irregular breathing (Cushing’s)
Why
You get head-injury signs: headache, vomit, slow heart, high wide BP, irregular breathing (Cushing’s).
11. Infant with a shunt problem — bulging fontanelle or sunken?
Answer
bulging fontanelle
Why
Infant clues include bulging fontanelle. Sunken fontanelle is dehydration, not pressure.
12. Trach tube out — what mask and bag on the stoma?
Answer
infant mask on an adult BVM
Why
Tube out: infant mask on an adult BVM, seal it over the stoma. Adult bag for volume.
13. Patient blue on a home vent — first move?
Answer
take them off the vent and bag them
Why
Person looks blue on a home vent: take them off the vent and bag them.
14. What does DOPE stand for?
Answer
displaced, obstructed, pneumo, equipment
Why
DOPE: displaced, obstructed, pneumo, equipment. Mucus is the most common obstruction.
15. Most common O in DOPE for a trach?
Answer
mucus / obstruction
Why
O — Obstruction. Mucus is the most common.
16. Central line or PICC pulled out — what do you do?
Answer
direct pressure, dry sterile dressing, closest ER
Why
If it pulls out or breaks: treat it like a wound. Direct pressure. Dry sterile dressing. Closest ER.
17. G-tube out — do you put it back in?
Answer
no — dry sterile dressing
Why
Tube out: dry sterile dressing. We do not put it back in.
18. Feeding-tube patient — suction where?
Answer
the mouth, not the belly hole
Why
Suction the mouth, not the belly hole.
19. Cerebral palsy — body, brain, or both assumption?
Answer
body-control problem; brain can be sharp
Why
Cerebral palsy: body-control problem. The brain can be sharp. CP: body, not always brain.
20. Newborn spina bifida — how do you cover the cord?
Answer
moist on the tissue, keep warm
Why
Treat like evisceration: moist on the tissue, keep the baby warm. Common latex allergy.
21. On the test, the weird history is often a decoy. What do you answer?
Answer
the ABC question
Why
The weird history is often a decoy. Answer the ABC question.
22. Do you replace trachs, G-tubes, or central lines?
Answer
no
Why
Do not replace trachs, G-tubes, or central lines. Bandage the hole. Manage the airway. Go.
