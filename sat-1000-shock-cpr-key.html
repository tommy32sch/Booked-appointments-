Types of shock and CPR fundamentals

Key notes. Saturday 10:00. 14-question recap.

Shock is a pump, pipe, or fluid problem. Name the type from one or two red-flag clues, then say if they are still compensating or crashing.

Compensated: adult systolic still ≥ 90. Heart rate and breathing are up. Skin is often cool, pale, and clammy. They can still look okay and be in trouble.

Decompensated (uncompensated on some tests): systolic under 90 in an adult. Compensation has failed.

Kids hold blood pressure longer, then crash fast. Capillary refill over 2 seconds is delayed and matters for shock in kids, even if pressure still looks okay.

Pipe problems (distributive)

Septic: severe infection. Fever or an infection source, hot skin, low pressure. You cannot give antibiotics. Standard shock care and tell the hospital possible sepsis.

Neurogenic: major spinal cord injury, often cervical. Low pressure with a slow pulse. Cool pale clammy above the injury, warm flushed below (a line of demarcation). Spinal immobilization (backboard, C-spine). Trauma center.

Anaphylaxis: allergic overreaction with two or more body systems (skin, breathing, circulation, gut). Adult epinephrine 0.3 mg IM in the lateral thigh. Pediatric 0.15 mg. Hold about 3 seconds. Oxygen before drugs, then shock care.

Psychogenic (fainting): brief vasodilation, they often wake when you arrive. Still a full medical eval. Check fall injuries. Do not call it true cellular shock.

Fluid problems (hypovolemic)

Hemorrhagic: blood loss you see or internal. Direct pressure then a tourniquet if you need it.

Non-hemorrhagic: dehydration (vomiting, diarrhea, no intake) or burns with third-spacing.

Pump and obstructive (overview)

Cardiogenic: weak pump, often a big MI. Chest pain, lung fluid, low pressure. No nitro if hypotensive. Aspirin only if indicated for MI.

Obstructive: tamponade (JVD, muffled tones, narrowing pulse pressure), tension pneumothorax (trauma, silent side, JVD), pulmonary embolism. You do not fix these. Shock care and rapid ALS transport.

Always

High-flow oxygen (non-rebreather or bag by breathing). Supine if they can. Keep warm even if they feel hot. Rapid transport. Reassess every 5 minutes if unstable. No food or drink. Request ALS for fluids.

CPR

Unresponsive and you think arrest: CAB, compressions first. Check breathing and pulse together, 10 seconds or less. Adults and children: carotid. Infants under 1 year: brachial. No definite pulse and not breathing normally: start compressions. Pauses 10 seconds or less.

AED looks for ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia. It reboots the chaos so a pacemaker can try. Asystole: no shock advised. Resume CPR right after a shock or a no-shock prompt.

Practice

  • Do this: say compensated vs decompensated with one adult number. You did it right if ≥ 90 is still compensating and under 90 is decompensated.
  • Do this: check cap refill on a classmate. You did it right if over 2 seconds is delayed for a kid in shock.
  • Do this: name neurogenic from trauma, low pressure, and a slow pulse. You did it right if you said neurogenic / cord injury.
  • Do this: name septic from fever, hot skin, and low pressure. You did it right if you said septic shock.
  • Do this: list two body systems for anaphylaxis, then say the adult epi dose and site. You did it right if you said 0.3 mg IM lateral thigh.
  • Do this: pick CAB vs ABC for an unresponsive, not-breathing patient. You did it right if you said CAB, compressions first.
  • Do this: show an infant pulse check. You did it right if you used brachial, with breathing, in 10 seconds or less.
  • Do this: say asystole on an AED. You did it right if you said no shock advised and you kept CPR going.
