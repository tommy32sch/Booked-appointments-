# Pulmonary embolism and obstructive shock — detailed

This was the short PE clip. A pulmonary embolism is a clot in the pulmonary circulation. It blocks blood flow to the alveoli. The trachea, bronchi, and bronchioles can still be open, so air still moves in and out.

That is why the lung exam can fool you. Breath sounds are air moving, not blood moving. You can hear clear, bilateral, normal sounds, see good chest rise, and still have a patient who is severely hypoxic.

Name the split. Ventilation is rate, rhythm, and quality of air movement. Respiration is gas exchange: oxygen into blood, carbon dioxide out. In a PE, ventilation can look fine and oxygenation is still failing. They get more cyanotic. The saturation falls.

The pattern to lock in: sudden difficulty breathing out of nowhere, maybe chest pain. Clear lungs. Good chest rise. Already on high-flow oxygen, a non-rebreather around 15 L/min. They still get bluer. The sat still drops. Breathing looks like it should be fixing the hypoxia, and it is not. Think pulmonary embolism.

Asthma and COPD usually bring wheezes and a longer expiratory phase. Fluid in the lungs (pneumonia or heart failure) usually brings crackles. Tension pneumothorax takes breath sounds away on one side. PE keeps the sounds.

PE, tension pneumothorax, and cardiac tamponade sit together as obstructive shock.

You do not fix the clot or the sac in the field. Treat for shock. Support airway, breathing, and circulation. Rapid transport. Paramedics may needle-decompress a tension pneumothorax. This recap does not give that to you as an EMT skill.

Tension extras: severe dyspnea, unilateral decreased or absent sounds, JVD, tracheal deviation late, hypotension.

Tamponade extras: JVD, hypotension, muffled heart sounds (Beck’s triad).

Give high-flow oxygen right away if they are in severe distress. Non-rebreather if rate and tidal volume are adequate. Bag them if breathing fails or they tire. Keep them warm. Position for status and comfort (supine or semi-Fowler if they tolerate it and are not hypotensive). Load and go. Tell the hospital you are worried about obstructive shock or a possible PE with hypoxia that will not rise.

If they are unstable, reassess every 5 minutes: level of consciousness, ABCs, vitals, saturation, skin. Write down the sudden onset, the clear lungs, the oxygen device and flow, and that the sat kept falling.

## Practice
Do this: write ventilation vs respiration in two short lines. You did it right if ventilation is air in and out and respiration is gas exchange.

Do this: run the PE pattern out loud. You did it right if sudden dyspnea, clear lungs, high-flow oxygen, and a falling sat are all there.

Do this: listen left and right and say PE vs tension. You did it right if equal clear sounds is PE and a silent side is tension.

Do this: name tamponade from JVD plus hypotension plus muffled tones. You did it right if you said cardiac tamponade.

Do this: say what you can and cannot fix. You did it right if you said you treat shock and transport, and you do not pull the clot or drain the sac.

Do this: pick NRB vs BVM. You did it right if adequate breathing gets a non-rebreather at about 15 L.

Do this: cover them and call the hospital line. You did it right if you said possible obstructive shock / PE with hypoxia that will not rise.

Do this: set a 5-minute reassess on an unstable classmate drill. You did it right if you recaptured mental status, ABCs, vitals, sat, and skin.
