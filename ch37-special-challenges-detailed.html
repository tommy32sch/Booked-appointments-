Special challenges — detailed

Ch 37. Fuller dump recap.

Light chapter. Peds and geriatrics feel like a review of the whole book. Do not get hooked by the rare history or the machines. The question is usually just ABCs.

How to work the call

Ask what you got called for. The Down syndrome or the vent may have nothing to do with the complaint.

Find their baseline. Not talking may be normal for them. Or it may be new and bad.

Life threat? Load and go. Not a life threat? Slow down. Bring the cane, the hearing aids, the service dog if you have time.

Blind: say everything before you touch them.

Deaf: write questions. Check that hearing aids are in and on. Whistling aid = not seated or a dead battery.

Service animal: if the patient is critical at home, leave the animal and arrange later. If they are alone at a store, the dog may come. Ask.

Start with the patient. Then ask the caregiver: is this normal for them? The family is often the expert on this person. Use them.

On the test, the weird history is often a decoy — answer the ABC question. Ask what is normal. Use the caregiver.

Down syndrome

Chromosome change. Mild to severe intellectual disability. Start with the patient. You will see the level fast.

Bigger tongue. Harder airway. Jaw thrust may be easier than a big head-tilt.

More seizures, heart defects, leukemia. Treat the complaint in front of you.

Seizure care does not change: protect them, suction, BVM if needed.

About 15% have an unstable C1–C2 joint (atlanto-axial). Higher spine risk in trauma.

Brain injury, CP, paralysis, spina bifida

Old brain injury: this is their baseline, not today’s wreck. Ask what is normal.

Cerebral palsy: body-control problem. The brain can be sharp. Do not assume they cannot think. CP is body, not always brain.

Paralysis: cannot move does not always mean cannot feel. Cord cut = both. Other causes may still feel.

Spina bifida (newborn): the cord is out the back. Treat like evisceration — moist on the tissue, keep the baby warm. Common latex allergy. Hydrocephalus is common.

Hydrocephalus and shunts

Too much CSF in the head. Usually a drainage problem, not over-production. A shunt is a pipe that drains that fluid.

  • VP shunt: brain ventricles → belly.
  • VA shunt: brain ventricles → heart.

If it clogs or infects, pressure builds. You get head-injury signs: headache, vomit, slow heart, high wide BP, irregular breathing (Cushing’s).

Infant clues: high-pitched cry, bulging fontanelle, projectile vomit. Sunken fontanelle is dehydration, not pressure.

Infection in a new shunt can go straight into the brain. An old shunt from years ago did not just get infected today.

Shunt failure looks like a head injury / Cushing’s.

Tracheostomy

Surgical hole in the neck into the trachea. Bypasses the nose and mouth. They lose warming, filtering, and humidifying. Mucus is the usual problem.

Tube out: infant mask on an adult BVM, seal it over the stoma (the hole). Adult bag for volume. Tiny mask to fit the hole.

Person looks blue on a home vent: take them off the vent and bag them. They improve → it was the machine. They stay bad → look at DOPE.

You may not need a head-tilt. The hole already bypasses the tongue.

We do not put tubes back in. Bandage the hole. Manage the airway. Go.

DOPE — tube or vent is not working

  • D — Displaced, dislodged, or damaged. We do not put the trach back in.
  • O — Obstruction. Mucus is the most common.
  • P — Pneumothorax.
  • E — Equipment failure (the vent itself).

Central lines, ports, PICC

The line ends near the heart. Infection goes straight into the blood. Do not touch it.

Even most paramedics start a new IV somewhere else. Arrest is the rare exception some medical directors allow.

If it pulls out or breaks: treat it like a wound. Direct pressure. Dry sterile dressing. Closest ER.

They fill the line with a blood thinner when not in use so it does not clot.

Do not replace central lines.

Feeding tubes (G-tube / NG / OG)

Through the mouth, the nose, or straight through the belly into the stomach. They cannot swallow safely.

Higher aspiration risk. Sit them up if you can. Suction the mouth, not the belly hole.

Tube out: dry sterile dressing. We do not put it back in. The hole starts to close like a piercing. Time matters, but it is not a 30-second killer.

Vomit blood = hematemesis. Fresh red or coffee-ground if it sat in the gut.

Do not replace G-tubes.

Ostomy bags

Ostomy = a surgical opening.

  • Colostomy = colon.
  • Ileostomy = small intestine.
  • Urostomy = bladder.

We do not change the bag. Look at the skin if infection might be the call. Red, hot, swollen = possible infection.

Keep bags off gurney wheels so they do not yank.

Big rules

Do not get hooked by the rare history or the machines. The question is usually just ABCs.

Start with the patient. Then ask the caregiver: is this normal for them?

We do not put tubes back in. Bandage the hole. Manage the airway. Go.

Vent not working? Take them off it and bag them.

Blue on a vent → bag them.

Trach out → infant mask + adult bag on the stoma.

Down syndrome: big tongue + unstable C1–C2.

Practice

Do this: open a special-challenges call. You did it right if you named what you were called for, checked baseline with the caregiver, and treated ABCs before the rare history.

Do this: blind and deaf approach. You did it right if blind meant say before touch, and deaf meant write questions plus check hearing aids in and on.

Do this: service animal decision. You did it right if critical at home → leave and arrange later; alone at a store → ask, the dog may come.

Do this: Down syndrome airway and spine talk-track. You did it right if bigger tongue, jaw thrust preferred, seizure care unchanged, and ~15% unstable C1–C2 for trauma.

Do this: name VP and VA. You did it right if VP drains ventricles to belly and VA drains ventricles to heart.

Do this: list shunt-failure vitals and infant clues. You did it right if Cushing’s pattern plus infant high-pitched cry / bulging fontanelle / projectile vomit, and sunken fontanelle meant dehydration.

Do this: trach-out skill words. You did it right if infant mask + adult BVM sealed on the stoma, adult bag for volume, and you did not put the trach back in.

Do this: blue-on-vent drill. You did it right if you took them off the vent, bagged, and only then walked DOPE if they stayed bad.

Do this: recite DOPE out loud. You did it right if displaced/dislodged/damaged, obstruction (mucus), pneumothorax, equipment failure.

Do this: PICC or central line pulled out. You did it right if direct pressure, dry sterile dressing, closest ER, and you did not use the line.

Do this: feeding-tube and ostomy station words. You did it right if sit up for aspiration risk, suction the mouth not the belly hole, dry sterile if tube out, and you do not change the ostomy bag.

Do this: newborn spina bifida care. You did it right if moist on the cord tissue, keep warm, latex allergy in mind, and hydrocephalus common.
