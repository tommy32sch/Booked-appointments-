Transport operations — key

Ops. Must-remember.

Real-world chapter. Driving is one of the most dangerous parts of the job.

The truck

State sets the minimum equipment list. Arizona can audit it unannounced. Extra toys (Lucas device) are the medical director’s choice.

Star of Life is on every side and the roof so a helicopter can see it too. Stickers on your personal car do not make you an emergency vehicle in Arizona.

Box is built for two EMTs and two supine patients. MCI may double or triple people in the back.

Bench is on the patient’s left. That is also why a late-pregnancy patient faces you on their left side.

Inside cabinets = patient care. Outside compartments = cones, vests, extrication.

Type 1: pickup / truck front + box on the back. A lot of local fire ambulances now.

Type 2: van. Around here that is often a wheelchair / non-emergent transport, not a 911 truck.

Type 3: van front + box on the back. The class ambulance is this.

Start of shift

You own the truck. Check it yourself. “The last crew said they restocked” will not save you.

Cleaning protects you from whatever they just hauled.

Put airway and suction near the head. Rare stuff (OB kit) can live in a high cabinet. You only need one OB kit. You need several NRBs.

Jump kit / 5-minute bag = what can kill them in the first five minutes. Fire here often just carries everything in.

Seatbelt the monitor and suction so they do not become missiles.

Driving

Lights and sirens add chaos. We ask people with a green light to stop so we can go through a red. That is a request, not a command.

Due regard: drive so you can still avoid a crash. Agency policy often caps at 10 over. 11 over + a wreck = they blame you.

Lights and sirens: both. Only courtesy exception some cities allow: empty road at 3 a.m., lights only.

Red light: stop. Eye contact. Lane by lane. The person with the green still has the right of way until they give it up.

School bus with the stop arm out: we stop too. Even if you are doing CPR in the back.

School zone: agency policy often kills the siren and does the posted speed.

Escorts: do not follow the fire truck blindly through the hole they made. They clear the intersection. Then you stop and clear it yourself. People restart on green after the first truck.

Backing: spotter every time. Even two feet. Cameras do not cancel that.

No extra distractions. Radio, map, and the partner in the back are already enough. No texting. No music with a patient on board. No eating.

Turns feel worse in the back. Slow down for your partner.

Parking on a wreck

100 feet. Same side of the road.

First unit: park BEFORE the wreck and block a lane. You are the shield.

Ambulance (usually second): park PAST the wreck so you can leave without pulling the shield away.

Intersection / no before or after: judgment. Close enough to work. Not in the fire department’s way.

On scene: lights on, siren off.

Hazmat: uphill and upwind. Tell other units which way to come in.

Helicopter

Fly for time and level of care, not miles. Stroke, heart attack, bad trauma, spinal cord, far-away burn center.

Make it medically and financially justified. Same-day discharge after a flight looks like a bad call.

Patient with capacity can refuse a flight. Educate. Do not kidnap.

Ask early. Standby = get ready, do not launch. Launch when you see it is real.

Let the pilot refuse for weather. That is their job, not yours.

Landing zone: 60 × 60 feet minimum. 100 × 100 is better. Weighted cones or vehicles at the corners. No tape. No flares. No loose junk.

Hot aircraft = rotors spinning. Stay away from the tail. Approach from the front with the flight crew. Downhill side on a slope.

Do not shine lights at the pilot. Light the trees and wires instead.

Arizona summer: thin air, tight weight. Tell them the estimated patient weight early so they can say no before they land.

Local crew is usually pilot + flight nurse + flight medic. They may tube on the ground because the cabin is tiny.

Hazmat: landing zone uphill and upwind. Patient must be decontaminated before they go in the bird. Same as before they go in your box.

Big rules

Due regard: if the crash could have been avoided, it is on us.

Lights AND sirens together. We request the right of way. We do not own it.

Stop at red lights. Clear lane by lane. A school bus with the stop arm out means we stop too.

You check the truck. Last crew saying they restocked does not protect your card.

Call the helicopter early. Let the pilot say no. Decon before the bird.

Practice

Do this: say due regard in one sentence. You did it right if an avoidable crash is on us, and you drive so you can still avoid a crash.

Do this: lights and sirens rule. You did it right if you said both together, and right of way is a request not a command.

Do this: red-light clearance talk-track. You did it right if stop, eye contact, lane by lane, and green still has right of way until they give it up.

Do this: school bus with stop arm out. You did it right if you stop too, even with CPR in the back.

Do this: escort through an intersection. You did it right if you do not draft the fire truck — they clear, then you stop and clear it yourself.

Do this: park on a wreck. You did it right if first unit parks before and blocks, ambulance parks past so it can leave, about 100 feet same side.

Do this: check-the-truck opening. You did it right if you said you check it yourself and last crew restock talk does not save you.

Do this: name LZ size. You did it right if 60 × 60 minimum, 100 × 100 better, weighted cones or vehicles, no tape/flares/loose junk.

Do this: approach a hot aircraft. You did it right if from the front with the flight crew, away from the tail, downhill side on a slope.

Do this: flight decision words. You did it right if fly for time and level of care, ask early, pilot says weather, you say weight, decon before the bird.
