# AED and CPR training — detailed

This talk is AED use and high-quality CPR, including kids and special pads.

The AED looks for ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia. Those are disordered electricity, so a shock can help. No shock advised means it is not VF/VT. Asystole is a dead battery; the shock button will not wake it. Resume CPR.

Keep compressing. Stop only when the AED says to for analysis or shock, or for the breaths in your ratio. The AED adds to CPR. It does not replace it.

Turn it on. Follow the prompts: expose the chest, stick pads on bare skin to the pictures, plug in if needed, stop touching for analysis. If you keep a hand on them, it can read your rhythm. Semi-automated AEDs charge, then you confirm everyone is clear and press shock. Fully automatic shock-without-you units are obsolete here.

Adult pads: upper right chest and lower left chest, along the heart’s usual path.

If the child or infant is too small or the pads are too large: one pad center chest, one center back. Pediatric pads tell the AED it is a child and drop the energy. No pediatric pads? Adult pads on the child. Extra energy beats no shock. Never pediatric pads on an adult.

Pacemaker or ICD is a bump under the skin. Do not park a pad on it. Shift about 1 inch (2–3 cm). Always look.

Pooled water conducts. Move them out. Sweaty or damp: wipe. They do not need to be bone dry. Metal under a pad (rails, heavy jewelry, underwire) can burn. Pull the bra if the wire is in the pad path. Peel a medication patch under a pad and wipe; adhesive can burn or ignite.

They have to be on their back on a firm flat surface. A mattress swallows the depth. Slide a board or move to the floor.

Rate 100–120. Faster than 120 hurts quality. Depth: adult at least 2 inches. Child about one-third of the chest (~2 inches). Infant about one-third (~1.5 inches). Let the chest come all the way back.

Adult hands: center of the chest, lower half of the sternum, heel of one hand, other on top, elbows locked, shoulders over the hands. Child: nipple line. Infant: just below the nipple line.

Alone: 30 compressions, 2 breaths, every age. Two people actually doing CPR: adults stay 30:2; children and infants 15:2 so the breather can see chest rise and not force air into a compressed chest. If the stem says your partner is getting the AED, you are still alone on CPR, so 30:2.

Each breath is a 1-second squeeze to visible rise. Pulse present but not breathing (adult): about 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 per minute).

## Practice
Do this: clear the patient out loud before a fake shock. You did it right if you said you are clear, they are clear, everybody is clear.

Do this: shift a pad 1 inch off a drawn pacemaker bump. You did it right if the pad is not on the device.

Do this: wipe damp skin and say they do not need to be bone dry. You did it right if you still moved them out of pooled water.

Do this: count 15:2 with a partner on a child, then 30:2 when you are solo. You did it right if the ratio followed who was actually doing CPR.

Do this: lock elbows and drop with your shoulders, not your arms. You did it right if the depth came from body weight.

Do this: let the chest recoil fully between compressions. You did it right if you were not leaning.

Do this: time breaths at one every 6 seconds for an adult with a pulse and no breathing. You did it right if you were near 10 breaths a minute.
