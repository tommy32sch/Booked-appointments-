Respiratory emergencies overview

Detailed notes. Saturday 3:55. 12-question recap.

This talk is field patterns: edema and CPAP, COPD, asthma, allergy vs anaphylaxis, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, PE, hyperventilation, carbon monoxide, and how you listen.

Pulmonary edema: fluid in the alveoli, usually from CHF. Dispatch is often difficulty breathing. If rate and tidal volume are adequate, start a non-rebreather. History of CHF, crackles, PND, orthopnea. Keep them sitting up. Lying flat spreads fluid over more lung.

CPAP: conscious, in distress, edema picture, still breathing. Inhalation pushes air in. Exhalation against pressure drives fluid back into the blood. Skip it for low pressure or shock (it drops pressure more), for anyone who cannot follow commands, for nausea, and for significant chest trauma. Recheck pressure and mental status.

COPD: chronic air trapping and high carbon dioxide. Normal people breathe from high carbon dioxide. Many COPD patients switch to hypoxic drive. On a short transport, still treat hypoxia. On a long ride, consider a cannula and medical direction, and their usual saturation.

Blue bloater is chronic bronchitis: infections, mucus, wheeze, baseline low oxygen, right heart failure, JVD, swollen feet. Pink puffer is emphysema: ballooned alveoli, less surface area, barrel chest, pursed lips.

Asthma is immune bronchoconstriction and mucus. Wheeze on the way out. Albuterol opens lower airways and also speeds the heart. Status asthmaticus needs ALS.

One-system allergy: hives and itch, stable. No epi. Two or more systems is anaphylaxis: skin plus breathing or pressure or gut. Epi. Oxygen, then epi. Scrape the stinger. Tweezers squeeze the sac. Up to about 20 minutes of extra venom.

Spontaneous pneumothorax: no trauma, tall thin male, smoker, cough, sudden sharp pain worse on inspiration. Sounds may still be normal if it is small. Watch for tension: JVD, low pressure, late tracheal shift. Oxygen, upright, go.

Pleural effusion: fluid outside the lung from infection or cancer. No crackles because the fluid is not in the alveoli. Decreased sounds over it. Field label may just be undifferentiated distress.

PE: clot from the legs through the right heart into the pulmonary arteries. Sudden dyspnea, maybe sharp pain, maybe blood-tinged cough. Sounds often normal. Saturation stays low on a non-rebreather because air arrives and blood does not.

Compensatory hyperventilation (diabetic ketoacidosis, aspirin overdose, some infections) blows off acid on purpose. Kussmaul is deep and fast. Do not just slow it. Panic hyperventilation makes them alkalotic: dizzy, tingling, clawed hands. Assess fully, including sugar. Box breathing. No paper bag.

Carbon monoxide binds hemoglobin harder than oxygen. The pulse ox still looks occupied, so it can read normal. Headache, several people sick, heaters, garage, fire. Get out. High-flow oxygen. Hyperbaric later.

Listen side to side. Snoring is tongue. Gurgling is suction. Stridor is upper. Wheeze is lower constriction. Crackles are alveolar fluid. Rhonchi are mucus in larger tubes. Absent on one side is pneumothorax or effusion.

PASTE: progression, associated chest pain, sputum, talking tiredness, exercise tolerance. Two- or three-word sentences is severe. Never force a sitting patient flat.

Practice

  • Do this: run PASTE out loud on a classmate. You did it right if all five letters got a question.
  • Do this: pick crackles vs no crackles for edema vs effusion. You did it right if crackles meant fluid in the alveoli.
  • Do this: refuse CPAP on a low pressure. You did it right if you said it drops pressure more.
  • Do this: coach box breathing on a panic drill, no bag. You did it right if the count was even and the bag stayed away.
  • Do this: name blue bloater vs pink puffer with one sign each. You did it right if JVD/edema vs barrel chest/pursed lips.
  • Do this: call ALS for status asthmaticus. You did it right if you did not wait for the inhaler to “maybe work later.”
  • Do this: put a CO patient on a non-rebreather even if the sat looks fine. You did it right if you said the probe cannot tell carbon monoxide from oxygen.
  • Do this: keep a pneumothorax patient upright and recheck for tension. You did it right if JVD and falling pressure were on your watch list.
