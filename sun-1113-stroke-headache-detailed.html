Stroke and headache overview

Detailed notes. Sunday 11:13. 12-question recap.

This talk is stroke recognition, last known well, and which headaches should scare you.

The brainstem keeps breathing, heart rate, and blood pressure going. The cerebellum is balance. The cerebrum is the bulk. Nerves cross, so the side of the brain is opposite the side of the body. Left frontal is speech.

Ischemic stroke is a clot, about 87 percent. Hemorrhagic is a burst vessel, about 13 percent, often chronic high pressure. You cannot tell them apart in the field. Hospital CT decides. Clot-busters and bleed care fight each other.

Find last known well. Thrombolytics often sit inside about 3 hours of onset. Cells start dying in about 10 minutes.

Smile. Arms out, palms up, eyes closed. Simple speech. Compare grips and legs. Tongue out. Ask what is new versus their old stroke. Sugar first. Seizure today. Head trauma. Blood thinners and AFib meds. Pre-notify. Go.

Hypoglycemia can copy the whole picture. Treat it. Recheck. Postictal, migraine, and a subdural or epidural bleed can copy too.

Headache: tension is dull squeeze. Migraine pounds with nausea and light. Nitro can cause a migraine-like ache by opening vessels. Sinus is pressure with a cold. Worst-ever sudden, or headache with fever, seizure, altered, trauma, or one-sided findings, is a brain problem until proven otherwise. No field headache drug. No over-the-counter advice. Position of comfort. Dim lights. Transport.

Practice

  • Do this: run last known well on three stories (witnessed, found down, old 1900 time). You did it right if each answer was last seen normal.
  • Do this: treat a low sugar that looks like a stroke, then recheck. You did it right if the deficits were gone after sugar.
  • Do this: pre-notify with age, findings, last known well, and sugar. You did it right if type of stroke was not in the guess.
  • Do this: hold aspirin on a facial droop. You did it right if you still gave oxygen to 94 to 99 percent.
  • Do this: ask smile, drift, and a set phrase in that order. You did it right if time came last as last known well.
  • Do this: flag a worst-ever headache with a seizure. You did it right if you did not call it a sinus headache.
  • Do this: refuse an ibuprofen suggestion. You did it right if they still got a hospital push.
  • Do this: name oxygen 94 to 99 percent on a stroke call. You did it right if you stayed in that band.
