Face and neck injuries — key

This chapter is mostly clues. Face trauma makes you think brain and C-spine. True killers here: airway and big bleeding. Eyes, teeth, and pretty bones wait until XABCs are done.

XABCs first. A broken jaw, tooth, or eyeball is not the next-minute killer.

No NPA on face or head trauma. Use a jaw thrust. An OPA is fine if there is no gag.

Eyes: moist dressing, cover BOTH. Do not push a globe back in.

Chemical in the eye: contacts out, force lids open, flush inside to outside at least 20 minutes.

Neck hole: pressure on one side only. Plastic to keep air out of the vessels. No tight collar if the neck is swelling.

Face hit → think brain. Head snapped back → think C-spine. The neck is packed and not protected by a rib cage: airway, carotids, jugulars, esophagus, C-spine. Face and scalp bleed and swell a lot. Ears do not. Air in an open neck vessel acts like a clot. Where the bubble sticks decides how bad it is. Most broken face bones are clues, not the problem. A broken jaw is a clue that the airway, brain, or neck may be hurt. A bump behind the ear is the mastoid. A bruise there is Battle’s sign — a skull-fracture clue.

Blood, swelling, and loose teeth can fill the airway at once. Open the airway with a jaw thrust when there is trauma or possible C-spine injury. NPA is out. The tube follows the roof of the nose. A cracked skull plus an NPA can go into the brain. A BVM would then blast air into the skull. OPA is still fine if there is no gag. A with-it patient can sometimes suction themselves. Wrap a bleeding scalp loosely. A tight wrap adds pressure inside a skull that may already be swelling.

Broken mandible (lower jaw) is easy to break because it sticks out. Teeth don’t line up, chin is numb, they cannot open the mouth. Worry about airway, brain, and C-spine. Broken maxilla (upper jaw) is harder — needs a straight-on hit. Same worries; the face can feel loose. A knocked-out tooth is an avulsion, not an amputation. Direct pressure on the socket. Suction if needed. Hold the tooth by the crown, not the root. Store in tooth solution, cold milk, or sterile saline. Replant window is 20 minutes to 1 hour. Do not delay the ambulance hunting for a tooth.

Every eyeball problem: moist sterile dressing so eyes stay wet. Cover BOTH eyes even if only one is hurt — eyes move together. Do not push a dangling globe back in. Moist gauze on it. Tape it so it does not swing. Lid cut only → light pressure if you are sure it is not the globe. Globe might be cut → do not press. Loose on the eye → flush from the nose outward. Impaled object → leave it; gauze doughnut; cover both. Heat burn → burning already stopped; do not force lids open; moist dressings; go. Chemical burn → still burning; contacts out; force lids open; flush inside → outside at least 20 minutes; after that long a flush, a dry dressing is allowed. Hyphema = blood in the front chamber in front of the iris; it can slosh. Do not dunk the whole face in a basin. Do not rip lids fused from heat.

Globe = eyeball, about 1 inch. Sclera = white outer coat. Choroid = middle (vessels). Retina = inner nerves. Optic nerve → occipital lobe. Front chamber = watery fluid the body can remake. Back chamber = jelly the body does not remake. Iris squeeze = pupil small. Anisocoria = unequal pupils (born that way, or treat as bad). Retina tear in a sudden stop can blind them. Fixed and dilated pupils in a code = brainstem not working.

Epistaxis = nosebleed. Most common cause: picking. Pinch the nose and lean FORWARD. Head back dumps blood into the throat. On a board they stay flat — watch the throat. Swallowed blood can come back as vomit. CSF leak from nose or ear means the skull is cracked. Halo test: blood in the middle, yellow ring = CSF.

Torn-off ear is an avulsion. Moist wrap → plastic → keep cool. Not bare ice. Ruptured eardrum: tinnitus (ringing), hard to hear, ear pain — a clue. Blast primary wave pops the eardrum; lungs are the dangerous hollow organ.

Impaled cheek: leave it if they are breathing fine and not dumping blood into the mouth. If it blocks the airway or they yanked it: gauze inside and outside, pinch. Awake patient holds it. Unconscious: you hold it and suction.

Blunt cracked larynx or trachea: every breath leaks air into soft tissue. Hoarse or no voice. Hard to swallow. Airway can swell shut. Subcutaneous emphysema feels like Rice Krispies (crepitus). You cannot poke the air out. Leaked air never reached the lungs. OPA and breathe for them if needed. They need surgery. Board them if the force was big. Skip the tight C-collar — the neck is already swelling.

Penetrating neck: bleed out, and air sucked into an open vessel. Pressure on the bleeding side only. Never wrap a full circle. Never squeeze both sides. Gauze on the wound, wrap under the opposite armpit. Plastic on top to keep air out of the vessels.

Practice

Do this: face trauma patient — think brain and C-spine, finish XABCs before jaw/tooth/eye. You did it right if airway and big bleeding came first.

Do this: open the airway with a jaw thrust; refuse NPA on face/head trauma; use OPA only if no gag. You did it right if cracked-skull + NPA → brain and BVM blasting air into skull was the reason.

Do this: wrap a bleeding scalp loosely. You did it right if tight wrap was refused because it adds pressure inside a swelling skull.

Do this: knocked-out tooth — pressure on socket, hold by crown not root, store in tooth solution / cold milk / sterile saline. You did it right if replant window was 20 minutes to 1 hour and you did not delay the ambulance hunting for the tooth.

Do this: moist sterile on the eye, cover BOTH, do not push a dangling globe — moist gauze and tape so it does not swing. You did it right if eyes-move-together was why both got covered.

Do this: chemical in the eye — contacts out, force lids open, flush inside→outside ≥20 minutes. You did it right if heat burn still had lids left alone and moist only.

Do this: flush something loose on the eye from the nose outward. You did it right if it did not run into the other eye.

Do this: epistaxis — pinch and lean FORWARD. You did it right if head-back dumping into the throat was refused.

Do this: penetrating neck hole — pressure one side only, gauze, wrap under opposite armpit, plastic on top. You did it right if full-circle wrap and both-sides squeeze were refused.

Do this: blunt cracked airway with Rice Krispies under the skin — OPA and breathe if needed, board if big force, skip tight C-collar. You did it right if leaked air never reached the lungs and poking air out was refused.
