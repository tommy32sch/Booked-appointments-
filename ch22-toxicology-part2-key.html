Toxicology part 2 — key

Most of these toxins have no EMT reversal. Keep the scene safe, do airway, breathing, and circulation (ABCs), and go to the hospital. Opioids are still the special case because you have naloxone (Narcan).

An unconscious downer is safer for you than an amped, paranoid upper. You do not know what an upper does next.

Restrain only if they are actively trying to hurt themselves or others. Not because they might. You can call medical control early and get permission “if needed.” That puts the decision on the doctor. Soft restraints are the default. Chemical restraint is an advanced life support (ALS) tool. Too much sedative swings them into a downer: breathing down, blood pressure down, heart down. Want police on these calls. Police decide arrest. Arrest means involuntary consent.

If Narcan wakes them and they refuse: push transport. Narcan wears off faster than the opioid still in the body. They can look fine, walk off, and go out again in about 30 minutes.

Inhaled leftover: is the gas still in the air? Your safety and hazmat first. Bad gas → give good gas: high-flow oxygen. Paint thinner, glue, and gasoline make the heart very irritable. Keep them calm. Do not jostle (same rule as severe hypothermia) or you risk ventricular fibrillation (VFib). Toxic inhale can flood the lungs with pulmonary edema. Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) smells like rotten egg. Cyanide smells like almond. Once you are safe, treat what you find: seizure, suction, bag-valve mask (BVM), cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR).

Stimulants copy fight-or-flight. Everything is UP: fast heart, fast breathing, high blood pressure, high energy, hallucinations, dilated pupils. Examples: phencyclidine (PCP), amphetamines, cocaine, meth. No reversal. Seizures, bad rhythms / heart attack, and stroke are more likely. Stimulant stroke is more likely a BLEED because blood pressure is so high. Cocaine can squeeze heart vessels (vasospasm) and cause a heart attack even without plaque.

Bath salts are synthetic cathinones. Paranoia and hallucinations can last up to 48 hours. Less common now. Safety plus ABCs plus hospital.

Marijuana: ABCs and transport. May be mixed with fentanyl — if they are sedated or out, try Narcan. Cannabinoid hyperemesis means lots of vomiting. Watch the airway. Days of vomiting can lead to hypovolemic shock.

Hallucinogens: ketamine, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), mescaline, mushrooms. A bad trip looks like fight-or-flight. Stay calm. Do not leave them alone. Call ALS if chemical restraint may be needed.

Cholinergic = parasympathetic = the brake. Adrenergic = sympathetic = the gas. Anticholinergic takes the foot off the brake → hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter. Examples: atropine, a lot of Benadryl, amitriptyline. Can seize and die in 30 minutes. No EMT reversal. Cholinergic stomps the brake → SLUDGE / DUMBELS: fluid from everywhere, slow heart, pinpoint pupils (miosis). Nerve agents and insecticides. Decontaminate first, then ABCs. Suction. DuoDote = atropine + pralidoxime (2-PAM). Not on every truck. Medics carry atropine.

Food: salmonella within 72 hours. Toxins from food left out hit faster, about 2–3 to 8–12 hours. Botulism = bad home-canned food / swollen can. Top-to-bottom paralysis. Kills breathing muscles. Starts in 24 hours to 4 days. Honey and infants. No EMT antidote. ABCs / BVM.

Plants: kids eat them. Do not memorize plant names. Call Poison Control. Take the plant or a photo to the emergency room (ER). ABCs and hospital.

Practice

Do this: say “no EMT reversal” for a stimulant, hallucinogen, or anticholinergic call, then list scene safe, ABCs, hospital. You did it right if opioids were the only Narcan exception.

Do this: choose soft restraints only when they are actively trying to hurt self or others. You did it right if “they might get combative later” was refused and medical control was OK early for “if needed.”

Do this: after Narcan wakes a refusal, push transport out loud. You did it right if you said Narcan wears off faster than the opioid and they can go out again in about 30 minutes.

Do this: for paint thinner / glue / gasoline, keep them calm and refuse rough moves. You did it right if VFib risk matched the severe-hypothermia rule.

Do this: name hydrogen sulfide as rotten egg and cyanide as almond. You did it right if high-flow oxygen followed safe air.

Do this: name a stimulant stroke as more likely a bleed, and cocaine vasospasm as a heart attack without plaque. You did it right if no stimulant reversal was claimed.

Do this: for heavy marijuana vomiting, protect the airway and ask how many days. You did it right if days of vomiting flagged hypovolemic shock risk.

Do this: recite hot / blind / dry / red / mad for anticholinergic and SLUDGE / DUMBELS for cholinergic. You did it right if decontaminate came before ABCs on cholinergic and DuoDote was atropine + 2-PAM.

Do this: name botulism as top-to-bottom paralysis from a bad / swollen can that hits breathing muscles. You did it right if honey and infants were listed and ABCs / BVM was the plan.
