AED and CPR training

Key notes. Saturday 11:30. 12-question recap.

The AED looks for shockable rhythms: ventricular fibrillation (VF) and pulseless ventricular tachycardia (VT). Shock advised means it found one. No shock advised means it is not VF/VT (asystole or another non-shockable rhythm). Shock will not help a flatline.

CPR is the priority. Keep compressions going except when the AED tells you to stop for analysis or shock, or for breaths in the right ratio.

Turn the AED on and follow the voice prompts. Hands off during analysis or it may read your rhythm. Modern units are semi-automated: clear the patient, then you press shock. Resume CPR right after a shock or a no-shock prompt.

Adult pads: one on the upper right chest, one on the lower left chest.

Child or infant too small for that: anterior-posterior (center of the chest and center of the back). Pediatric pads drop the energy. No pediatric pads? Use adult pads on the child. Do not put pediatric pads on an adult.

Pacemaker or ICD bump: move the pad about 1 inch (2–3 cm) off the device.

Pooled water: move them out. Damp skin: a quick wipe is enough. Remove a medication patch under a pad and wipe. Remove an underwire bra if the wire sits under a pad.

Patient supine on a firm flat surface. Rate 100–120 per minute (not just “at least 100”). Adult depth at least 2 inches (5 cm). Child about one-third of chest depth (~2 inches). Infant about one-third (~1.5 inches). Full recoil. Adult hands: lower half of the sternum. Child: nipple line. Infant: just below the nipple line.

Single rescuer: 30:2 for every age. Two people actually doing CPR: adults stay 30:2; children and infants go 15:2. If your partner is only grabbing the AED, you are still a single rescuer, so 30:2. Breaths: 1 second, just to chest rise. Pulse but not breathing (adult): about 1 breath every 6 seconds.

Practice

  • Do this: say shockable vs no-shock. You did it right if VF/pulseless VT get a shock and asystole does not.
  • Do this: hands off for analysis and say why. You did it right if you said the AED can pick up your rhythm.
  • Do this: place adult pads upper right and lower left on a classmate (clothes on is fine for the drill). You did it right if the path is top right to lower left.
  • Do this: name front-back placement when pads are too big. You did it right if one pad is center chest and one is center back.
  • Do this: compress to a metronome at 110. You did it right if you stayed between 100 and 120.
  • Do this: run 30:2 as a single rescuer on a child scenario. You did it right if you did not switch to 15:2 while you were alone.
  • Do this: switch a child to 15:2 only when two people are actually doing CPR. You did it right if the extra pair of hands is on compressions and breaths, not just fetching the AED.
  • Do this: give one 1-second breath to visible rise. You did it right if you did not blast the bag.
