# Face and neck injuries — detailed

This chapter is mostly clues. Face trauma makes you think brain and C-spine. True killers here: airway and big bleeding. Eyes, teeth, and pretty bones wait until XABCs are done.

## The big rules
XABCs first. A broken jaw, tooth, or eyeball is not the next-minute killer. No NPA on face or head trauma. Use a jaw thrust. An OPA is fine if there is no gag. Eyes: moist dressing, cover BOTH. Do not push a globe back in. Chemical in the eye: contacts out, force lids open, flush inside to outside at least 20 minutes. Neck hole: pressure on one side only. Plastic to keep air out of the vessels. No tight collar if the neck is swelling.

## What this chapter is for
Face hit → think brain. Head snapped back → think C-spine. The neck is packed and not protected by a rib cage: airway, carotids, jugulars, esophagus, C-spine. Face and scalp bleed and swell a lot. Ears do not. Air in an open neck vessel acts like a clot. Where the bubble sticks decides how bad it is. Most broken face bones are clues, not the problem. A few bones are themselves a disaster. A broken jaw is a clue that the airway, brain, or neck may be hurt. A bump behind the ear is the mastoid. A bruise there is Battle’s sign — a clue to a skull fracture. Head and spine care come next.

## Airway first
Blood, swelling, and loose teeth can all fill the airway at once. Open the airway with a jaw thrust when there is trauma or possible C-spine injury. NPA is out. The tube follows the roof of the nose. A cracked skull plus an NPA can go into the brain. A BVM would then blast air into the skull. OPA is still fine if there is no gag. A with-it patient can sometimes suction themselves. You have to trust they can protect their own airway. Wrap a bleeding scalp loosely. A tight wrap adds pressure inside a skull that may already be swelling.

## Jaw and teeth
Broken mandible (lower jaw): easy to break because it sticks out. Teeth don’t line up, chin is numb, they cannot open the mouth. The bone will heal. Worry about airway, brain, and C-spine. Broken maxilla (upper jaw): harder to break. Needs a straight-on hit. Same worries plus the face can feel loose. Knocked-out tooth: it is an avulsion, not an amputation. Direct pressure on the socket. Suction if needed. Bring the tooth. Hold it by the crown, not the root. Store in tooth solution, cold milk, or sterile saline. Replant window is 20 minutes to 1 hour. Do not delay the ambulance hunting for a tooth.

## Eyes
Not usually a next-minute killer. Blindness is a life-changing problem. Finish XABCs first.

Globe = the eyeball, about 1 inch. Bones hide most of it. Needs a hit straight-on. Sclera = white outer coat. Choroid = middle (vessels). Retina = inner nerves. Optic nerve → occipital lobe (back of the brain). Front chamber = watery fluid (body can remake). Back chamber = jelly (body does not remake). Iris is the colored muscle. Squeeze = pupil small. Relax = pupil big. Pupils are a brainstem check. Fixed and dilated in a code = brainstem not working. Anisocoria = unequal pupils. A few people are born that way. If you do not know that, treat it as bad. Write what you see. Retina can tear off in a sudden stop (same idea as the third crash in a car). Can blind them.

Every eyeball problem: moist sterile dressing. Eyes stay wet. Cover BOTH eyes even if only one is hurt. Eyes move together. Covering both keeps the hurt one still. Do not push a dangling globe back in. Moist gauze on it. Tape it so it does not swing.

Cut on the lid only: light pressure if you are sure it is not the globe. Globe might be cut: do not press. Pressure can squeeze the inside out. Something loose on the eye: flush from the nose outward so it does not run into the other eye. Impaled (nail, hook): leave it. Does not block airway or CPR. Gauze doughnut. Cover both. Heat burn: burning already stopped. Do not force lids open. Moist dressings. Go. Chemical burn: still burning. Contacts out. Force lids open. Flush inside → outside at least 20 minutes. After that long a flush, a dry dressing is allowed. Hyphema: blood in the front chamber, in front of the iris. It can slosh if they move their head. Do not dunk the whole face in a basin. Chemical will run into the mouth, nose, and other eye. Do not rip lids that are fused from heat.

## Nose
Epistaxis = nosebleed. On a call just say nosebleed. Most common cause: picking (“digital trauma”). Front bleeds are easier. Back bleeds are harder to stop. Pinch the nose and lean FORWARD. Head back dumps blood into the throat. On a board they stay flat. Watch the throat. Swallowed blood can come back up as vomit.

CSF is fluid made in the brain. If it leaks from the nose or ear, the skull is cracked. Halo test: drop on gauze. Blood clumps in the middle. Yellow ring around it = CSF. You do not fix the crack here.

## Ear
Stuck out, so it gets hit. Does not bleed much. Torn off: it is an avulsed ear. Moist wrap → plastic → keep cool. Do not set it on bare ice (frostbite; no blood is warming it). Ruptured eardrum: ringing (tinnitus), hard to hear, ear pain. Clue, not the killer. Blast: primary wave pops the eardrum easily. Lungs are the dangerous hollow organ.

## Impaled cheek
Leave it if they are breathing fine and not dumping blood into the mouth. If it blocks the airway, or they already yanked it: gauze inside and outside the cheek, pinch. Awake patient can hold it. Unconscious: you hold it and suction.

## Neck — blunt
Cracked larynx or trachea: every breath leaks air into the soft tissue. Hoarse or no voice. Hard to swallow. Airway can swell shut. Subcutaneous emphysema: air under the skin. Face and eyes can blow up. Feels like Rice Krispies. That feel is also called crepitus (usually crepitus means a broken bone). You cannot poke the air out. The real problem is that leaked air never reached the lungs. EMT care: OPA and breathe for them if they need it. More air will also leak. They need surgery. A paramedic tube past the hole can bypass the leak. Board them if the force was that big. Skip the tight C-collar. The neck is already swelling.

## Neck — penetrating
Two killers: bleed out, and air sucked into an open vessel. Pressure on the bleeding side only. Never wrap a full circle. Never squeeze both sides. Gauze on the wound, wrap under the opposite armpit. Plastic dressing on top to keep air out of the vessels.

## Must-remember clues
Face trauma = think brain and C-spine. Mastoid bruise = skull-fracture clue. No NPA. Jaw thrust. OPA if no gag. Broken jaw is a clue, not the emergency. Tooth: crown not root. Saline or milk. 20 minutes to 1 hour if they can replant. Eyes: moist, cover both. Globe stays out. Chemical 20 minutes, contacts out. Epistaxis: pinch and lean forward. Halo ring = CSF = skull crack. Air under the neck skin = cracked airway. No tight collar. Neck hole: one-sided pressure + occlusive. Ear torn off is an avulsion. Bring it cool, not on bare ice. Head and spine come next.
