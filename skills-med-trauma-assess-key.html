Medical and trauma assessment — key

Skills lab. Oral-station drills.

Oral stations. You talk the call. About 15 minutes. Most people finish in about 10 if they stay on the sheet. You get a blank page or a whiteboard. Three attempts. Trauma is first on skills weekend. Medical is usually after the written final.

One proctor. One scenario. You ask. They only give what you ask.

Script the first 30 seconds the same way every time.

You may use notes. Wean off the printed skill sheet before test day.

Pass the written first when you can. A fail on the final usually means a fail on medical too. Do not burn an attempt.

This is PEMDAS for a call. Order of operations. Do not skip lines.

Opening script — both stations

Say this in one breath. Do not think about it.

BSI. Is my scene safe?

What is the nature of illness? (Trauma: what is the mechanism of injury?)

How many patients?

I am going to request additional EMS / consider ALS.

I am going to consider C-spine.

They will give you one patient. Ask anyway. Consider C-spine even on chest pain. Just say it.

BSI and scene safety is the first sentence of every station. Say it out loud.

Primary — both stations

General impression: what do I see, hear, and smell when I walk in? Eyes open or closed. Position. Color. Work of breathing. Blood on the floor.

Level of consciousness: alert and tracking → AOx4 (person, place, time, event) if you need it. Eyes closed: verbal first. Then pain. Do not start with a sternal rub. AVPU. Unresponsive = they failed every step.

Chief complaint = what THEY say hurts most. It may not be the real problem.

X = huge bleed, sucking chest, anything that kills in the next minute. Treat it now.

Find a life threat → treat it now. Then put a mental check on that letter and keep walking.

XABC — treat as you find

X — Spraying blood. Direct pressure. Still spraying on a limb → tourniquet. Sucking hole → gloved hand, then plastic 3 sides.

A — Talking = patent. Unconscious = open it. Trauma = jaw thrust. Vomit / blood / teeth / gurgling = suction ≤ 10 seconds. No gag → OPA. Gag / no face trauma → NPA.

B — Rate, rhythm, quality. Chest rise both sides? Rate 8 and shallow → BVM, 15 L, one breath every 6 seconds. Unequal + hard to bag → note it. Keep bagging.

C — Pulse rate, rhythm, quality. Skin color, temp, condition. Cool pale clammy + fast weak pulse = shock. High-flow oxygen, keep warm, load and go.

Every letter gets a mental check mark. Skip a life threat and that is a fail point.

Medical sheet — the extra work

After ABCs and the transport decision you still owe four things trauma does not.

History. SAMPLE. If it is pain, add OPQRST.

Secondary exam that matches the complaint (chest, belly, neuro).

Full vitals: BP, pulse, respirations, SpO2, BGL, pupils, skin.

Field impression: “I think this is a heart attack / overdose / low sugar.” Not a hospital diagnosis. Then say the treatments.

Call medical control for drugs unless they tell you that you have standing orders. On written tests the safe answer is usually call.

Reassess. Unstable every 5 minutes. Stable every 15.

Medical is the hard one. You must say a field impression and the treatments.

Trauma sheet — what changes

MOI instead of NOI. C-spine is more often real.

X shows up more: bleed, sucking chest, flail, unstable pelvis.

Signs of shock → say the shock package out loud: oxygen, supine, warm, rapid transport.

Order of vitals vs secondary can swap; do not forget vitals. The sheet writes vitals first.

No field impression. No “I’m calling the hospital to tell them my diagnosis.”

Life threats now. Broken tibia later. Note it. Splint on the ride if you have time.

Board them when you move. Scan the back while you roll.

Trauma is XABC + a detailed head-to-toe. No field impression. No “call the hospital to tell them my diagnosis.”

DCAP-BTLS — say the letters

Deformities. Contusions. Abrasions. Punctures. Burns. Tenderness. Lacerations. Swelling.

Extra looks at each stop

Scalp and behind the ears. Battle’s sign. Fluid from ears or nose.

Face bones. Pupils. Blood in the mouth.

Neck: trachea midline or pushed. JVD. Step-off on the C-spine.

Chest: crepitus, bruising, hoop marks. Listen both sides.

Belly: all four quadrants. Rigid + bruised = hidden bleed.

Pelvis: gentle in, gentle down. Unstable → binder when you can.

Genitals / perineum: blood.

Legs then arms: bones, distal pulses. Unresponsive = pulses only. No motor/sensory.

Back: already cleared when you boarded.

Rodeo demo — what “good” sounded like

Prone, unresponsive, blood in the mouth, gurgling → roll, jaw thrust, suction, OPA.

Rate 8, shallow, one side down → BVM now.

Pulse 172 weak. Cool pale clammy. BP 72/50. Shock package + board + go.

Trachea starting to shift + silent one side → tension in your head. Keep bagging. We do not needle.

Rigid bruised belly + unstable pelvis + closed tib-fib. Binder the pelvis. Note the leg. Do not stop the truck for a pretty splint.

Reassess every 5. Airway, bag, shock. That is the job.

Traps

Written question “what first?” = this sheet’s order, not “do everything at once.”

You do not have to name every injury out loud. Naming tension / basilar / belly bleed helps the written test. Missing the treatment fails the skill.

Three attempts is not a plan. Pass the first time.

Practice

Do this: say the opening script in one breath. You did it right if BSI/scene safe, NOI or MOI, how many patients, request EMS/consider ALS, consider C-spine — all out loud.

Do this: walk XABC out loud while treating as you find. You did it right if every letter got a mental check and a life threat was fixed before you moved on.

Do this: medical extras after ABCs. You did it right if you said SAMPLE (± OPQRST), matching secondary, full vitals, field impression + treatments, and reassess 5/15.

Do this: trauma shock package out loud. You did it right if oxygen, supine, warm, rapid transport.

Do this: DCAP-BTLS letter drill. You did it right if deformities, contusions, abrasions, punctures, burns, tenderness, lacerations, swelling.

Do this: trauma head-to-toe extras. You did it right if Battle’s/CSF, trachea/JVD/step-off, both-side chest listen, four belly quadrants, gentle pelvis, binder if unstable.

Do this: rodeo airway sequence. You did it right if roll, jaw thrust, suction, OPA for prone unresponsive gurgling blood in the mouth.

Do this: rate 8 shallow unequal chest. You did it right if BVM now at 15 L, one breath every 6 seconds, and you kept bagging even if tension was in your head.

Do this: vitals vs secondary on trauma. You did it right if order can swap and you still got vitals.

Do this: refuse field impression on trauma. You did it right if you treated life threats and did not call the hospital to announce a diagnosis.
