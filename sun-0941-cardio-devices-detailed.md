# Cardiovascular emergencies and devices — detailed

This talk is high pressure, a tearing aorta, hardware on the heart, and how you run an AED around that hardware.

A systolic over 180 plus headache, bounding pulse, ringing ears, nosebleed, nausea, dizziness, or altered mental status is a hypertensive emergency. Arteries can burst. Stroke and aortic dissection sit on that list. Nitroglycerin is not your blood-pressure drug here. It drops fast, then rebounds, and the rebound is the rupture window. Calm, quiet, position of comfort, go.

Aneurysm is ballooning. Dissection is blood splitting the wall. Rupture dumps the whole cardiac output out the hole. Sudden tearing chest to the back between the shoulder blades, maximal at the first second, maybe different arm pressures. Crushing pressure that builds is the MI wording from this recap, not this picture. Oxygen, still, rapid transport.

CABG is a grafted artery sewn on. Stent is a metal scaffold after a balloon. History of either is heavy heart disease, not a cure. Chest pain still counts.

Pacemaker: lump near the clavicle. Fixed-rate keeps a set beat. Demand waits and fires when needed. Failure brings back the old slow rhythm. Treat the shock. You cannot fix the pacemaker.

ICD shocks inside. Safe to touch. Arrest still gets CPR and an AED. A vest talks, then hits with a real external shock. Hands off for that one.

VAD: cable out of the body to a box and batteries. Continuous flow often means no pulse. Talking equals perfusion. Unconscious: breathing, then the device, then the phone number on the controller. That team tells you CPR or not and which hospital. Do not lose the spare batteries.

Shockable: ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia. Asystole is a dead battery. Pulseless electrical activity has electricity and no pulse. No shock advised is not a death sentence and is not proof they are back. Check the pulse. Kids: breathe for them. Hair: shave or wax with the first pads. Metal they lie on matters more than a ring. After they come back, 1 breath every 6 seconds for an adult, every 2 to 3 seconds for a child. Keep the pads on.

## Practice
Do this: run the three dissection questions on a classmate. You did it right if sudden, tearing, and to the back all got asked.

Do this: name nitro rebound as the reason you do not drop pressure with it. You did it right if you still treated chest-pain nitro only when protocol allows.

Do this: point to a clavicle lump as a pacemaker or ICD. You did it right if pads would go beside it, not on it.

Do this: clear a talking vest. You did it right if you waited out the countdown.

Do this: inspect a VAD box for power and alarms before you start compressions. You did it right if a talking patient never got CPR for a missing pulse.

Do this: leave AED pads on after a shock. You did it right if you expected another analyze in about two minutes.

Do this: pick no-shock plus a pulse as ROSC. You did it right if you then checked airway and breathing.

Do this: shave or rip-and-replace pads on a hairy chest. You did it right if you did not skip the AED for hair.
