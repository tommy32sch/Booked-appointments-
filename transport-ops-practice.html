1. What does due regard mean for ambulance driving?
Answer
drive so you can still avoid a crash
Why
Due regard: drive so you can still avoid a crash. If the crash could have been avoided, it is on us.
2. Agency speed cap often mentioned for emergency driving?
Answer
10 over
Why
Agency policy often caps at 10 over. 11 over + a wreck = they blame you.
3. Lights and sirens — one, the other, or both?
Answer
both
Why
Lights and sirens: both. Lights AND sirens together.
4. Going through a red on lights and sirens — request or command?
Answer
request, not a command
Why
That is a request, not a command. We request the right of way. We do not own it.
5. At a red light with lights and sirens, who has the right of way until they give it up?
Answer
the person with the green
Why
The person with the green still has the right of way until they give it up.
6. School bus with the stop arm out — what do you do?
Answer
we stop too
Why
School bus with the stop arm out: we stop too. Even if you are doing CPR in the back.
7. Following a fire escort through an intersection — what is the mistake?
Answer
following blindly / drafting the fire truck
Why
Do not follow the fire truck blindly. They clear. Then you stop and clear it yourself. Do not draft the fire truck.
8. Backing the ambulance — when do you need a spotter?
Answer
every time, even two feet
Why
Backing: spotter every time. Even two feet. Cameras do not cancel that.
9. First unit on a wreck — where do you park?
Answer
BEFORE the wreck and block a lane
Why
First unit: park BEFORE the wreck and block a lane. You are the shield.
10. Ambulance (usually second) on a wreck — where do you park?
Answer
PAST the wreck so you can leave
Why
Ambulance (usually second): park PAST the wreck so you can leave without pulling the shield away.
11. About how far from a wreck, and which side?
Answer
100 feet, same side of the road
Why
100 feet. Same side of the road.
12. Hazmat approach — uphill/upwind or downhill/downwind?
Answer
uphill and upwind
Why
Hazmat: uphill and upwind.
13. Who checks the truck at start of shift?
Answer
you — check it yourself
Why
You own the truck. Check it yourself. Last crew saying they restocked will not save you.
14. Minimum landing zone size? Better size?
Answer
60 × 60 minimum; 100 × 100 better
Why
Landing zone: 60 × 60 feet minimum. 100 × 100 is better.
15. How do you approach a hot aircraft?
Answer
from the front with the flight crew
Why
Stay away from the tail. Approach from the front with the flight crew. Downhill side on a slope.
16. Fly the helicopter for miles, or for what?
Answer
time and level of care, not miles
Why
Fly for time and level of care, not miles.
17. Standby vs launch for flight — what is standby?
Answer
get ready, do not launch
Why
Standby = get ready, do not launch. Launch when you see it is real.
18. Who refuses the flight for weather?
Answer
the pilot
Why
Let the pilot refuse for weather. That is their job, not yours.
19. Arizona summer flight — what do you tell them early?
Answer
estimated patient weight
Why
Tell them the estimated patient weight early so they can say no before they land.
20. Before a hazmat patient goes in the helicopter — what must happen?
Answer
decontaminated / decon
Why
Patient must be decontaminated before they go in the bird. Same as before they go in your box.
21. Type 3 ambulance is what layout?
Answer
van front + box on the back
Why
Type 3: van front + box on the back. The class ambulance is this.
22. Bench seat is on which side of the patient?
Answer
patient’s left
Why
Bench is on the patient’s left. That is also why a late-pregnancy patient faces you on their left side.
