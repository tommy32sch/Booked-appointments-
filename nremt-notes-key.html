Chapter 1 — EMS systems

Chapter 1: EMS Systems — NREMT key (drill)

Sources: HIS Ch 1 lecture + study guide only.

[Scene Size-up and Safety]

  • Be safe before responding / getting to the patient.
  • Scene-size-up steps / named hazards / PPE: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Operations]

  • 1966 White Paper: high trauma deaths → modern EMS
  • 1970 NHTSA under DOT
  • 1971 AAOS first EMT textbook
  • White Paper title beyond “White Paper”: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Shift: pure transport → healthcare + medical oversight
  • EMR ~50 h: CPR + first aid
  • EMT ~130–150 h: BLS, limited meds
  • AEMT: IVs + expanded meds; hours not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Paramedic ALS ≥1,000 h: advanced airways, EKG, 55–60 meds
  • EMT + paramedic work under Medical Director’s license; MD is legally responsible
  • AZ historically EMT + Paramedic only; EMR/AEMT recent, limited jobs
  • Urban: advanced skills usually paramedic-only
  • Rural: some trained EMTs start IVs with medical director approval
  • Skills-based: know right, do wrong (O2 2 LPM not 15)
  • Rules-based: right rule, wrong situation (stroke as low sugar)
  • Knowledge-based: new + incomplete → wrong plan (wrong antidote)
  • Primary prevention: stop before it starts (vaccines, seatbelts, education)
  • Secondary: detect early (screenings; early HTN/cholesterol Rx)
  • Tertiary: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • Offline = standing orders, no phone: CPR/AED, O2, epi for anaphylaxis; most routine
  • Online = radio/phone; one situation only; new call each time; unsure / unusual / extra auth
  • Advocacy: best interest; speak up; honor DNR/wishes; explain; privacy/dignity; report errors
  • Capacity → may refuse Rx and transport even if badly hurt (partially amputated leg)
  • Assess capacity carefully; document everything
  • Later unconscious / lose capacity → implied consent → treat and transport
  • Capacity test steps / extra consent types: not in his notes / don’t invent.

Chapter 2 — Workforce safety and wellness

Chapter 2: Workplace Safety and Wellness — NREMT key (drill)

Sources: HIS Ch 2 lecture + study guide only.

[Scene Size-up and Safety]

  • You first. Cannot help if you become a patient.
  • Front-door survey: look, listen, smell, mood. Re-scan after every task. Trust your gut.
  • Police: violence / weapons / threats / crime / aggressive people / suspected abuse.
  • Hazmat: spills, unknown substances, chemical fumes, toxic exposure, industrial hazmat.
  • Pathogen / contamination / exposure / transmission.
  • Routes: direct, indirect, droplet, airborne (TB, measles), vector.
  • Standard precautions every call. Always: hand hygiene + gloves. Add eyes/mask/gown/N95 as needed.
  • Sanitizer ≥60% if not dirty. Soap/water if soiled or norovirus / C. diff.
  • Don: hygiene → gown → mask → eyes → gloves. Doff: gloves → gown → eyes → mask → hygiene.
  • N95 = airborne + fit-tested. Surgical mask = droplets. Never recap needles.
  • Match other providers’ PPE level on scene.
  • Safety order: you (gloves every time) → crew → patient.
  • Park 100 ft fires, crashes, hazmat. First unit upstream; later downstream.
  • Hazmat: uphill / upwind. NREMT: ERG.
  • If not safe, do not enter.

[Primary Assessment]

  • Fight-or-flight: sympathetic + stress hormones.
  • Fast HR/BP, fast breathing, dilated pupils, blood to muscles/heart/lungs/brain, slow digestion, tense, sweat, less pain, heightened senses.
  • Can look like shock (pale/cool/sweaty, nausea, anxiety).
  • Named hormones: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Operations]

  • Acute (during/right after) vs delayed (days–months: nightmares, flashbacks) vs cumulative (burnout).
  • Goal: prevent PTSD/burnout. CISM steps: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Infection-control plan: exposure determination, compliance methods, Hep B vax, post-exposure follow-up, training, records/sharps log, cleaning.
  • Exposure: hand off care → wash/flush → do not squeeze/suck/bleach → report immediately → eval within hours → PEP if indicated, no cost to you.
  • Cannot force new source blood in most cases. Exact PEP drugs/hours: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • Critical patient: introduce, orient, honest not cruel, hope without false hope, notify family.
  • Grief: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance — not always in order.

Chapter 3 — Medical, legal, and ethical

Chapter 3: Medical, Legal, and Ethical Issues — NREMT key (drill)

Sources: HIS Ch 3 lecture + study guide only.

[Operations]

  • Good faith + appropriate standard of care → usually avoids legal exposure.
  • Standard of care = reasonably prudent EMT, similar training, same situation.
  • Negligence = duty + breach + damages + causation (all four).
  • Abandonment = leave without consent and without equal/higher handoff while they still need care.
  • Confidentiality: care team / required by law (GSW, abuse, public health, crimes) / court / written permission.
  • Not family (unless allowed), not media, not social.
  • HIPAA by name / off-duty duty-to-act: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • Expressed = alert + capacity + agree (verbal/nonverbal), must be informed.
  • Informed = complete expressed (nature, risks, benefits, alternatives). All informed is expressed; not all expressed is informed.
  • Implied = unresponsive/altered, true emergency, reasonable-person assumption.
  • Involuntary = legal authority (hold, court, danger to self/others).
  • Capacity → refuse even if they may die. No capacity + emergency → implied, treat.
  • Valid refusal: capacity, informed, voluntary, signature when possible.
  • Capacity test: understand, appreciate consequences, reason, communicate a choice.
  • Before leaving: reassess, confirm, encourage 911, responsible person, sign or document refusal to sign, med control if required.
  • Living will often not enough for EMS to withhold. Need valid DNR/POLST.
  • Conscious capacity overrides paper. Doubt → treat + med control.
  • AZ DNR form rules / hold statutes: not in his notes / don’t invent.

[Scene Size-up and Safety]

  • Restraint last resort: danger, protect, allow care, less-restrictive failed.
  • Never prone, never hog-tie. Soft = EMS preferred. Chemical = ALS, not normal EMT.
  • Monitor ABCs. Med control if possible. LE when possible.

[Primary Assessment]

  • Presumptive ≠ withhold (unresponsive, no breathing, no pulse, fixed dilated pupils).
  • Definitive: lividity, rigor, decomposition, incompatible injuries — or valid DNR/POLST.
  • Doubt → CPR.
  • ME: unexpected, trauma/violence/suicide, suspicious, no recent physician. LE first. Don’t move the body.

Chapter 4 — Communications and documentation

Chapter 4: Communication and Documentation — NREMT key (drill)

Sources: HIS Ch 4 lecture + study guide only.

[Secondary Assessment]

  • Open-ended first, then closed-ended.
  • Confrontation / interpretation / explanation / summary — know the examples.
  • Avoid: false reassurance, leading questions, advice, interrupting, jargon, judgment, talking over the patient.
  • Children / special needs: their level, involve caregivers, simple language.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • 10 golden rules: eye contact; names; truth; plain language; careful talk about them; body language; slow/clear; face hearing-impaired; time to answer; calm confidence.
  • Transfer only after verbal handoff to equal or higher + completed PCR.

[Operations]

  • Radio: brief, plain English, no patient name, repeat orders back.
  • SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation/ETA.
  • PCR is legal + permanent. Include demographics, CC, history, vitals, findings, treatments, response, times. Facts only. Not documented = didn’t happen.
  • PCR error: one line, initial, date, correct. Never erase.
  • Refusal on PCR: capacity, risks, understanding, signature or refusal to sign.
  • 10-codes / CHART/SOAP / ePCR steps: not in his notes / don’t invent.

  • Document found/transported position.

Saturday — Circulatory + airway

Saturday AM: Circulatory + Airway — NREMT key (drill)

Sources: HIS 8:02 intro, 8:33 fundamentals, 9:02 live note, 9:32 airway, combined pack only.

[Scene Size-up and Safety]

  • Scene safety always first. You become a patient → you help nobody.
  • Adult: go to patient first. Child: go to parent first if present.

[Primary Assessment]

  • Pump + pipes + fluid. Fail any one → no circulation/BP. Goal: O2 + sugar to every cell.
  • HR 60–100 adult rest (avg 70). >100 tachy, <60 brady. Athletes in 40s can be OK.
  • SV = one beat. CO = SV × HR (one minute). Field: BP, skin, mentation — no volume gadget.
  • 80 mL × 70 = 5.6 L/min. Body ~5–6 L. Three 2-L sodas.
  • Aorta first. Rupture → fatal in seconds (as he said).
  • Bleed → low preload → low BP → can pass out. Brain hates low O2, sugar, temperature.
  • Afterload = pipe size / SVR. Constrict ↑P, dilate ↓P. Anaphylaxis = dilated; Epi constricts.
  • Pulse uses: dead/CPR vs rate vs distal to injury. Pulse = LV wave.
  • BP: top = LV pump (ex. 120); bottom = LV relax/fill (ex. 80). Cuff steps: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Arterial: bright + spurting. Venous: darker + ooze. IV = vein.
  • ABC first. No airway → no good patient. Fix A before you move on.
  • Most common block: tongue (unconscious).
  • Trauma head/neck/spine: jaw thrust, no head tilt. Medical: head-tilt chin-lift.
  • OPA = no gag. NPA = has gag; measure nose → earlobe, lube, twist.
  • Look, listen, feel. SpO2 goal ≥94%.
  • NC 2–6; NRB 10–15 (most patients); BVM 15 if inadequate/apneic.
  • Suction out only, only while pulling stuff. Adult 15 / child 10 / infant 5 s.
  • Recovery: unconscious and breathing → side.
  • Distress = still moving air. Arrest = stopped. Treat the same: open, O2, assist, watch SpO2, listen.

[Secondary Assessment]

  • Pulmonary = to lungs. Systemic = to body. RA deox → RV → lungs → LA ox → LV → body.
  • Cardiac muscle makes its own electricity. Unconscious ≠ heart stops.
  • Atria together, then ventricles. LV thicker. Weak LV → backup to lungs / SOB (CHF preview).
  • Valves TPMA: tri (R, 3) → pulmonic → bi/mitral (L, 2) → aortic. Sounds = slam shut. MVP = inverts.
  • SVC top of RA; IVC bottom. Pulm arteries deox away; pulm veins ox back.
  • SA node = primary pacemaker (sinus). AV = pause to fill. EKG read = medic.
  • Arteries away. Deox arteries: pulmonary (+ fetal umbilical arteries). Umbilical vein is ox.
  • Coronaries off aorta feed the muscle. Clog = MI. Chamber blood does not feed the heart.
  • Hepat- = liver; detox; trauma = big bleed (~40% volume preview). Renal = kidneys filter.
  • Mesentery suspends gut + vessels.
  • RBC ~120 days. Bones make. Spleen kills old ones (not “filter” like kidneys).
  • Gas exchange only at capillaries. Meet alveoli. Diffusion.
  • Sites: carotid, radial, femoral, brachial vs humerus, top of foot, behind/inside ankle bump. Latin names: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Solid (spleen, liver, pancreas, kidneys) = bleed. Hollow (e.g. stomach) = infection.
  • Quadrants: cross at belly button. Patient’s L/R. Spleen = LUQ. Full organ lists: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Plasma + formed elements: RBC, WBC, platelets.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • Blood thinners = no clot. Good for clot risk, bad if bleeding. Daily BP meds can blunt fight-or-flight.
  • EpiPen job he named here: massive vasoconstriction.
  • O2 unless a reason not to (reasons: not in his notes / don’t invent.).
  • Disease-specific Rx (asthma, COPD, CPAP, nebs): not in his notes / don’t invent.

Saturday Ch 8 moving — NREMT key (drill)

[Operations]

  • Study when to use / not use each device. Not a huge national-test chapter.
  • Gurney: all transports; not stairs / not off-road; head-first; head heavier; lock to floor; everyone on the gurney.
  • Powered 40–145 lb. Two hands (front + back). Curb straight.
  • Backboard = full-body splint. X torso + hips + legs. 6–7 ft plastic.
  • Board if: neck/back pain; numbness/tingling/weakness/paralysis; unconscious trauma; poor historian.
  • Too wide: might worsen — justify or med control.
  • Log roll: four people, head in charge, collar first, don’t lift by the head.
  • Lift with legs; shoulders over pelvis; power grip palms up ~10 in.
  • Lift assist = extra people, not a second ambulance. His shop ~250 lb two-person.
  • Bariatric problem is width (regular ~750 lb; he said ~1200). Keep low. Lift-gate or ramp + winch.
  • Diamond carry: 4 sides preferred.
  • Stair chair: conscious, no spine, no elevator. Unconscious stairs: board, feet first.
  • Emergency = seconds, skip spine (fire / pulseless in wreck / CPR).
  • Urgent ≈ one minute, still incomplete immobilization (~2+ min even when fast).
  • Non-urgent = stable; least lift.
  • Vest/KED-type: sitting in car, rare, stable + conscious + C-collar.
  • Vacuum mattress: beanbag + suction; kyphosis; rare locally; unsure → med control.
  • Stokes: raised sides, water/rope/Camelback. Board-in-basket possible.
  • Scoop: split; hip fx; 50%/12 mo stat he heard.
  • Isolette: neonate 0–1 month; flight crew’s patient; he just drove / secured the vehicle.

[Patient Treatment and Transport]

  • No medical need → position of comfort.
  • Shock four: lots of O2, warm, supine, rapid transport.
  • Shock now flat (no feet-up).
  • Pregnancy >20 weeks = left side (IVC/aorta sandwich = supine hypotensive syndrome; also faces the bench).
  • Unresponsive + breathing + no spine = recovery (tongue).
  • Seizure recovering + breathing = recovery; else back + suction.
  • Fowler / high / semi = sit; dyspnea likes up.
  • Restrain only if hurting self/others; 5 people; supine not prone; rule out O2/sugar/head first; document or it reads like kidnapping; monitor ABCs.

[Scene Size-up and Safety]

[Primary Assessment]

  • Five pieces: scene → primary (life threats) → history → secondary (exam) → reassess.
  • Primary = biggest NREMT bucket + most class time. He said ~35–45% of the test.
  • Begins when you greet / get eyes on; snap “how bad.”
  • Seven steps: general impression → LOC → airway → breathing → circulation → rapid exam (more trauma) → priority / transport (go now vs stay).
  • Rule: find a life threat → treat that life threat.
  • GI: awake / unconscious / dead / head-bob about to drop; age, sex, race, distress, appearance, position (couch clutch, face-down, tripod), sounds.
  • First GI question: could they be dead? If maybe → pulse / CPR now. Awake + looking = not dead. GI can be wrong (mute stroke; “out” was asleep).
  • Bright-red spurting bleed: treat now even if it “belongs” later on the sheet.
  • AVPU (taught):

- A = awake/alert, looking around, sees you walk in. Slumped couch = not awake. - V = any response to “Hey sir, my name’s Andrew…” (hey or eyes open). - P = pain/pressure: sternal rub (knuckles), trap pinch, nail-bed squeeze. Any moan / swat / turn-away = pain. - U = no response to pain either → unresponsive. No further checks. - Stop when you get a level. Do not cause pain if already A or V.

  • A&O×4 (if A): person / place / time / event. His usual: name, where, year, president. Short/medium/long-term memory. A&O×4 ≈ capacity (not a guarantee — “always/never/guarantee” are test traps). Miss one (Bush / 1945) → implied consent unless circumstantial (driving through, doesn’t know city; retiree vs day-of-week; Jan 1 year lag). Unsure → treat or med control.
  • Not A&O×4 (or not their baseline) = altered. Ask what is normal; nobody present → assume worst, hope best.
  • Immediate life threats he listed: airway obstruction (tongue #1; also food/choke), resp failure (still breathing but not enough — e.g. 6/min shallow OD), resp arrest (not breathing), shock/hypoperfusion, severe bleed, cardiac arrest. Distress (complains of SOB but moving plenty of air) is not an immediate life threat — not on that list.
  • Airway goal: patent = open/clear. Talking or crying = airway good → move on. Unconscious: assume the tongue. Find an airway problem → stop and clear it before later steps.
  • Open: head-tilt chin-lift if NO spine worry; jaw-thrust if trauma/spine (underbite; stabilize cheeks; fingers at angle of jaw; push jaw up; tongue comes with jaw; don’t crank the neck). Then look inside; blood/vomit out.
  • Four airway steps: open, assess, clear, maintain (adjunct later / tomorrow Ch 11).
  • Sounds: normal breathing is quiet. Gurgling → suction. Snoring. High-pitched whistle.
  • Breathing after airway is open. Rate / rhythm / quality. Chaotic irregular → brainstem / head injury. Depth = chest rise (same metric if you ventilate, bystander mouth-to-mouth, tube, or trach).
  • Adult: consider PPV if >28 or <8. Kids: younger = faster baseline — skew numbers (he did not give kid cutoffs). Slow almost always ventilate; fast is a maybe (could be helping; panic hyperventilate — don’t walk up with an airway device).
  • SpO2 goal ~94–99%. Pulse ox = % of hemoglobin “seats” occupied; can be falsely skewed — one piece of the picture.
  • Work of breathing (not why): accessory muscles, retractions (skin in between ribs / around clavicle), nasal flaring, 2–3 word dyspnea (dys = difficulty, pnea = breathing), tripod, sniffing (straighter airway path), labored. Extra O2 device tomorrow.

  • Pulse site: conscious → radial. Unresponsive child/adult → carotid. Infant unconscious → brachial (carotid pressure could collapse infant airway; after one year structures solid enough).

- Snoring = tongue (#1). Gurgling = fluid (sometimes “bubbling” on a test). Stridor = upper swelling/constriction, high-pitched whistle (bad allergy). - Wheeze = lower constriction, high-pitched mostly exhalation, classic asthma. - Crackles = fluid / pulmonary edema (drown or body fluid into alveoli) — straw-in-water bubbling. - Rhonchi = mucus / congestion, low-pitched rattling, “junky,” classic pneumonia. [Primary Assessment]

  • After safety + “alive,” airway is top of patient care. Only thing that may beat it: major life-threatening bleed seen on general impression → treat the life threat.
  • Chapter focus = primary steps 3 and 4: airway + breathing.
  • Dispatch “difficulty breathing” ≠ diagnosis. After ABCs you may land cardiac; chest pain may land respiratory. Systems intertwined.
  • Upper airway = above larynx / vocal cords. Jobs: warm, filter, humidify. Parts he named: nasopharynx, oropharynx, epiglottis (covers larynx so food does not go to lungs).
  • Lower airway = below larynx. Job: gas exchange. Path: larynx → trachea → carina (bifurcation) → R/L bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli (grape sacs) meet pulmonary capillaries.
  • Mediastinum = middle space (heart, trachea, proximal bronchi, aorta, SVC/IVC). Lungs sit in pleura.
  • Phrenic nerve → diaphragm. C3, 4, and 5 keep the diaphragm alive. High C-spine (at/above that exit): paralyzed and cannot breathe. Below C5: still paralyzed but can breathe.
  • Heart makes its own electricity. Every other muscle (including diaphragm) needs a brain/nerve shock.
  • Order: ventilation → respiration → oxygenation (then circulation).

- Ventilation = mechanical air in and out. - Respiration = gas exchange. - Oxygenation = O2 on hemoglobin.

  • Evaluate that order. Ventilation OK (ex. 18/min + good chest rise) but blue → not a vent problem → think respiration (listen; fluid/edema) → then pulse ox → then circulation/cardiac.
  • Gases do not diffuse well through fluid. Pulmonary edema = brick wall: O2 in + CO2 out both fail.
  • Inhalation = active. Diaphragm drops, intercostals expand cage → container bigger → negative intrathoracic pressure → air pulled in. Normal breathing = negative pressure.
  • We cannot make that vacuum → we do positive pressure ventilation (push). Opposite of the body → air also goes down esophagus → stomach air → later vomit.
  • Lungs do nothing if diaphragm/intercostals cannot contract (even “world’s healthiest lungs”).
  • Diaphragm = voluntary and involuntary.
  • Exhalation should be passive (relax → container smaller → air out). Asthma / COPD: extra work to get air out → fatigue.
  • Tidal volume = one breath. We do not measure mL. Proxy: chest rise and fall. Too small TV → air may never reach alveoli → no gas exchange.
  • Inspiratory / expiratory reserve: extra you can force in/out. Residual volume keeps alveoli open (he used these; Hering–Breuer stretch reflex stops overinflation when their brain is in charge).
  • When you ventilate: nothing stops overinflation except your hand. Gentle squeeze just until chest rise, then stop. “Half a bag” = he does not know what half looks like or their lung size. Be the calm / zen person on scene.
  • Primary breathing trigger: CO2 too high (chemoreceptors in blood + CSF). Brain → phrenic → diaphragm.
  • Backup: hypoxic drive (wait for O2 low). COPD next week: CO2 always high → brain switches to O2 drop. Disease Rx: not in his notes / don’t invent.
  • Gas exchange needs both air in alveoli and blood in pulmonary capillaries (clot in lung = no blood to exchange).
  • Aerobic = enough O2 → lots of energy (ATP = “weird way to spell energy”) + waste CO2 + water + heat. Anaerobic = not enough O2 → less energy + lactic acid. Supply vs demand (Ch 17 heart-attack preview).
  • Diffusion = high → low, seeks balance.
  • External / pulmonary respiration = lungs (O2 into blood). Internal / systemic respiration = cells (O2 off hemoglobin into the cell).
  • Intrinsic vent problems: infection, allergy (swelling), unresponsive tongue. Extrinsic: trauma swelling, blood in airway.
  • Intrinsic respiration problems: pneumonia mucus, pulmonary edema, COPD airflow. Extrinsic: low atmospheric / partial pressure of O2 (Everest). Room air ~21% O2; percent stays ~21% at altitude — quantity / pressure drops, not the percent.
  • Pneumothorax = air in chest outside the lung (collapsed lung). Hemothorax = blood in chest. Both usually trauma (rib, GSW, or we overinflate). Hemo-pneumo = both. EMT cannot decompress. Medic = big needle = short-term (hole still leaks → surgery). Speed depends on hole size. Both lungs holed: survive possible if fast surgery.
  • Anemia = not enough RBCs (bleed or production).
  • Adequate breathing (adult): 12–20/min, equal rise/fall, regular pattern, clear equal bilateral sounds, adequate depth = chest rise. Adequate = enough air × enough times/min. Can still have SOB, hypoxia, cyanosis, SpO2 91%.
  • Inadequate = not moving air well enough → you breathe for them (too slow; too shallow).
  • Test split: device choice is almost always adequate vs inadequate (50/50 if you read R/R/Q).
  • Abnormal rate: <12 or >20 adult. 22 with good rise, regular, clear = abnormal-fast but may still be adequate (change % O2, not take over). Slow usually means ventilate. Irregular often not adequate (head trauma).
  • Accessory use, retractions (skin in at ribs / clavicle), skin CTC = circulation first but clue respiratory.
  • Agonal gasps → link to heart stopped / CPR. Not a true sign of life; not functional air. Couch-gasp while looking at you ≠ that phrase.
  • Cheyne–Stokes: slow/shallow → deeper/faster → slow → apnea → repeat. Pattern you can follow. Brainstem / severe head trauma.
  • Biot / ataxic: no pattern. Chaos. Same context.
  • Kussmaul: deep, rapid. Metabolic acidosis compensation (blow off CO2). DKA (Ch 20), aspirin OD (acetylsalicylic acid), tricyclic antidepressants. Do not slow this down. Yesterday’s “consider PPV if >28” — fast is a maybe; body may be helping.
  • Apnea = not breathing = respiratory arrest. Dyspnea = difficulty breathing.
  • Even if ventilating well, respiration can fail (18 + rise + lungs full of fluid + cyanosis + SpO2 80%).
  • Brain is O2-sensitive → confusion → unresponsive fast. Skin (blue) + pulse ox.
  • ETCO2 / capnography = more medic; book talks a lot. 35–45 he said do not need to remember. Capnometry = number; capnography = waveform. Needs medic monitor. Shark-fin = asthma (hard to get air out). Good exhaled CO2 = vent + resp + ox + circ + cells using O2. Pulse ox only proves in + exchange + saturation; the rest is assumed. [Primary Assessment]

  • Talking / kid wailing = airway check, move to breathing.
  • Unconscious → muscles relax → tongue = #1 obstruction. Do not judge breathing until the path is open (they may breathe as soon as the tongue is off).
  • Unconscious airway work: patient supine, you at the top of the head. Awake patients already sit up and protect themselves.
  • Two opens, same job (tongue off the throat):

- Head-tilt chin-lift: one hand on top, one under chin, tilt back. Needs confidence there is no spinal injury. Easier to hold. Examples: found in bed; seen slump on couch (no crack on the floor). - Unknown / possible spine: jaw thrust. Thumbs on cheekbones, fingers behind angle of the jaw, push jaw up (underbite). Head/C-spine stay still. If you let go, jaw + tongue drop. You are stuck holding it. - Don’t know (unconscious on sidewalk, maybe hit by a car) → err caution → jaw thrust.

  • Four steps: open → assess → clear → maintain. Only then “airway check,” then breathing R/R/Q.
  • Open the mouth if needed. Muscles are loose — just open it. Book cross-finger / scissor = book answer; he never uses it and avoids fingers in the mouth (bite story).
  • Assess = look inside + listen. See fluid/blood/vomit/secretions or hear gurgling → suction. Test strategy: do not add gurgling that is not in the stem; do not skip suction if the stem says gurgling.
  • Aspiration into lungs: mortality +30–70% on top of the original problem.
  • Suction not ready → immediately roll to the side (gravity). Fastest move is often hands / position (same idea: sit the SOB patient up). Then set up gear. Then back supine for the rest of care.
  • Units: hand-pump (cramps; stuff drains back if you stop) = backup; battery portable (what they take on scene; check charge at start of shift); wall in the ambulance / hospital.
  • Indication = see it or hear it. Nothing seen/heard → do not pick “suction.”
  • Catheter + tubing = sterile wrap, single-patient, trash. Canister cap → biohazard. Machine = spray disinfect like the gurney. Keep a packaged set with the unit so you are not hunting.
  • Tubing: thick-wall, non-kinking (vacuum should not collapse it).
  • Rigid hard plastic = go-to for mouth junk (bigger hole). Flexible / non-rigid = smaller hole, clogs on chunks. Uses he named: down a trach or down a medic ET tube. Registry words: rigid suction catheter / non-rigid suction catheter (not Yankauer / French / whistle-tip brand names on the test).
  • Sizes: rigid “pretty standard.” Flexible only slightly shorter / fatter / thinner. No real size menu for gag. not in his notes / don’t invent French sizes.
  • Sterile water jug: suction water between passes so blood/vomit does not clot/dry the line.
  • Start-of-shift: attachments on; test vacuum. Book: more than 300 mmHg. Gage, light, or test on your glove if no gage (their red units).
  • Suction sequence: on → test → measure corner of mouth → earlobe or corner of mouth → angle of jaw → insert only that far / only as far as you can see (not into trachea) → insert with suction OFF (side hole open so vacuum is not at the tip) → cover the hole → circular motions on the way out → flush water → repeat as needed.

  • They are probably not breathing adequately (could not clear themselves) → need positive pressure depth, not just a nose cannula / simple mask (he shut that idea down).
  • Pulmonary edema = fluid in the lower airway. You only suction what you see/hear in the upper airway. Lower-lung fluid = different intervention later. not in his notes / don’t invent.
