# Chest injuries — key

Chest holds airway, breathing, and circulation. Surgery is the real fix. Platinum 10 minutes. XABCs first. Board later.

Hole in the chest wall: plastic dressing (occlusive / chest seal / 3-sided). Gauze lets air through. Seal any hole from the sternal notch to the belly button, front and back. “Convert open to closed” = seal it. In-and-out wound: seal both holes. If the seal clogs: peel it, let it vent, reseal.

Tension pneumo vs hemothorax look the same except the neck veins. Fat veins / JVD = air (tension). Flat veins = bled out (hemo). Both: chest trauma, shock, quiet on one side. Crackles are the wrong clue for hemo — that blood is outside the lung; crackles mean fluid inside the alveoli. Hemo needs surgery and blood. Go.

Tension vs tamponade: both shock + JVD. Tamponade has two good lungs and a narrow pulse pressure. Tension has quiet one side and trachea pushed away from the bad side. We do not needle. Paramedic needle is for a closed tension pneumo only.

Flail chest: do not tape a bulky dressing on it. Do not use sandbags. That is the old answer. ABCs, oxygen, shock care, go. Watch for a bruised lung underneath.

Irregular pulse after a steering-wheel hit = bruised heart (cardiac contusion). Kid drops after a ball to the chest = commotio cordis. CPR + AED.

## Anatomy must-remember

Box = ribs + spine + diaphragm. Upper-belly stab still gets a chest seal — you do not know where the diaphragm was. Adults breathe with diaphragm + intercostals. Kids are almost all diaphragm. Under each rib: nerve, vein, artery.

Parietal pleura = lining of the chest wall. Visceral pleura = lining on the lung. Pneumothorax = air in that gap. Hemothorax = blood in that gap. Mediastinum = middle space: heart, aorta, vena cava, trachea, esophagus.

Phrenic nerve = C3, C4, C5 — keeps the diaphragm alive. Cord cut above C5 → they do not breathe. Below C5 → diaphragm still works, intercostals do not.

## Breathing words

Ventilation = air in and out (rate, rhythm, quality — what you count). Respiration = gas exchange in the alveoli. Oxygenation = oxygen onto red cells, then circulation delivers it. They can breathe 20 times a minute with good rise and still be blue. Crackles = fluid in the alveoli (water or blood). Body sucks air in (negative pressure). BVM pushes air in (positive pressure), so some air also goes to the stomach.

## Signs you will see

Open = hole in the skin. Closed = skin intact. Crepitus = broken-rib grind. Hemoptysis = coughing blood = lung. Hematemesis = vomiting blood = gut. Pink frothy sputum = pulmonary edema. One-sided chest rise or quiet lung = that lung is not filling. Paradoxical motion = one segment moves the wrong way (flail) — not the whole side. Default shock in trauma is bleed. Then think obstructive: tension pneumo or tamponade.

## Contusions and other chest hits

Pulmonary contusion: blood in the alveoli, crackles, can take hours, may kill days later. No CPAP in trauma. BVM only if they are not breathing well. Cardiac contusion: steering wheel + shock + irregular pulse. Sternal fracture: the heart is two inches behind it. Clavicle can poke the top of the lung.

Traumatic asphyxia: huge crush shoves blood up into the head — purple / bruised face and neck, bloodshot whites of the eyes, JVD. ABCs and go.

Commotio cordis: blow during the heart’s recharge → V-fib. Classic kid baseball. CPR + AED if they drop. On the EKG the hit is during the T wave (repolarization). Normal beat: P → QRS → T. Force fire while still recharging → V-fib.

## Add-on: pulse pressure and aorta

Cushing / ICP: high and WIDE pulse pressure (example 200/90). Tamponade: NARROW and low — the two numbers squeeze together.

You cannot pinch the aorta in the chest the way you tourniquet a limb. No access unless the chest is already wide open. Those bleeds need the hospital / surgery. Torn aorta from trauma is often dead on scene. Medical aneurysm (not burst): tearing chest pain. Hospital mesh sleeve / sock around the aorta is not the same as a bypass graft.

## Practice

Do this: seal an open chest hole with plastic (occlusive / chest seal / 3-sided or non-porous dressing). You did it right if gauze was refused, both holes got sealed on an in-and-out wound, and a clogged seal was peeled, vented, and resealed.

Do this: tell tension from hemo by the neck veins. You did it right if fat veins / JVD meant tension and flat / collapsed veins meant hemothorax, with crackles refused as a hemo clue.

Do this: spot tamponade vs tension. You did it right if both had shock + JVD, tamponade had two good lungs and a narrow pulse pressure, and tension had a quiet side / trachea away.

Do this: care for flail chest. You did it right if bulky dressing and sandbags were refused, and ABCs / oxygen / shock care / go were the plan.

Do this: kid drops after a ball to the chest. You did it right if CPR + AED started fast for suspected commotio cordis / V-fib.

Do this: compare pulse pressures. You did it right if Cushing / ICP was high and wide (e.g. 200/90) and tamponade was narrow and low.

Do this: pulmonary contusion breathing care. You did it right if CPAP was refused in trauma and BVM was used only if they were not breathing well.
