Toxicology part 2 — detailed

This is the second half of toxicology: inhaled leftovers, stimulants, marijuana, hallucinogens, restraints, anticholinergics versus cholinergics, food poisoning, and plants. Most of these have no EMT reversal. Keep the scene safe, do airway, breathing, and circulation (ABCs), and go to the hospital. Opioids stay the big exception because you have naloxone (Narcan).

The big safety picture: an unconscious downer is safer for you than an amped, paranoid upper. A person out cold is not going to hurt you. A person who is amped, paranoid, and seeing things might. You do not know what they do next.

Restrain only if they are actively trying to hurt themselves or other people. Not because they might get combative later. You can call medical control early and get permission “if it becomes needed.” That puts the decision on the doctor. Combative right now means you and/or police restrain. Soft restraints are the default. Chemical restraint is an advanced life support (ALS) tool. A tiny sedative dose can take the edge off. Too much swings them into a downer: breathing down, blood pressure down, heart down. Want police on these calls. If they are not there, ask dispatch to send them. Police decide arrest. Arrest means involuntary consent. If police have not arrested them, they are not under arrest. Confused means no capacity, so implied consent. Oriented but clearly on something and refusing: if you do not feel safe leaving them, call medical control. Do not kidnap. Do not walk away from someone you think will get hurt.

If Narcan wakes them and they want to leave, that is a capacity question. Default: push transport. Narcan wears off faster than the opioid still in the body. They can look fine, walk off, and be unconscious again in about 30 minutes.

Inhaled toxins leftover from part 1: first ask if the gas is still in the air. Your safety and hazmat come first. They inhaled bad gas → give good gas. High-flow oxygen. Paint thinner, glue, and gasoline can make the heart very irritable. Struggling or moving them roughly can throw the heart into ventricular fibrillation (VFib) / arrest. Same rule as severe hypothermia: keep them calm. Do not jostle. Toxic inhale can also flood the lungs (pulmonary edema). The body tries to wash the chemical out. Fluid blocks gas exchange. Hydrogen sulfide smells like rotten egg; it hits the lungs and is sometimes used for suicide. Cyanide smells like almond. Once you are safe, treat what you find. Seizure → treat it. Secretions → suction. Bad breathing → bag-valve mask (BVM). Arrest → cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR). Nausea is common. End path for many poisons: seizure, coma, death.

Stimulants (uppers) copy fight-or-flight. Everything is UP: fast heart, fast breathing, high blood pressure, high energy, hallucinations, dilated pupils. Examples: phencyclidine (PCP), amphetamines, cocaine, meth. They can be swallowed, injected, or smoked. No reversal. ABCs and safe transport. The hospital rides it out. What can go wrong: seizures from an irritated brain; bad heart rhythms / heart attack; stroke — more likely a BLEED because blood pressure is so high. Cocaine and other stimulants squeeze heart vessels (vasospasm) and can cause a heart attack even without plaque.

Bath salts are synthetic cathinones. Was trendy; less common now. Paranoia, fast heart, hallucinations, headache. Can last up to 48 hours. No special EMT drug. Safety plus ABCs plus hospital.

Marijuana: relaxed, drowsy, short-term memory trouble, confusion. ABCs and transport. Nothing special. Could be mixed with fentanyl. If they are sedated or out, try Narcan. Safe to try. If Narcan does nothing, it is not opioids. If breathing gets better but they stay out, something else is still on board. Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome means vomiting a lot. Classically after a lot of edibles or long-term use; other routes are possible. Treat like any heavy vomiting: watch the airway. Ask how much and how long. One hour is not the same as days. Days of vomiting can cause hypovolemic shock.

Hallucinogens change the senses. Visual hallucinations. Pulled away from reality. Names from the notes: ketamine, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), mescaline, mushrooms (psilocybin). LSD picture: wires crossed — hear colors, taste music. A bad trip looks like fight-or-flight. No reversal for you. Usually none at the hospital either. Ride it out. Stay calm and professional. Do not leave them alone. Call ALS if you may need chemical restraint.

Anticholinergic versus cholinergic. Cholinergic is another name for parasympathetic (rest and digest) = the brake. Adrenergic is another name for sympathetic (fight or flight) = the gas. Anticholinergic takes the foot off the brake. Only gas is left. Looks like fight-or-flight: high blood pressure, fast heart, fast breathing, tight vessels, big pupils. Can go from normal to seizure and death in 30 minutes. Mnemonic: hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter. Blind means pupils blown, too much light. Red means flushed from high blood pressure / shunting. Dry means no sweat. Examples: atropine (medics carry it), a lot of Benadryl, amitriptyline (an antidepressant). No EMT reversal. ABCs and hospital.

Cholinergic stomps the brake. Slows the body and dumps fluid from everywhere. Military: nerve agents. Civilian: insecticides / pesticides. Garden center = think hazmat. Fluid from everywhere plus slow heart plus pinpoint pupils (miosis, same word as opioids). Two mnemonics, same idea: SLUDGE and DUMBELS. Think diarrhea, urination, saliva, sweat, tears, snot, drool, slow heart, pinpoint pupils. Suction the airway. Fluid loss can cause shock. Decontaminate first. Then ABCs. DuoDote is two drugs in one auto-injector: atropine plus pralidoxime chloride (2-PAM). Shot in the thigh, like an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen). Not on every ambulance. Every medic carries atropine (also used for heart rhythms). 2-PAM is more a hazmat-team or military self-dose drug. Medic idea for atropine here: give some, then keep doubling until they stop leaking fluid.

Food poisoning is either the germ itself or toxins the germ made. The body hates it and flushes both ways: vomit and diarrhea. Same worries every time: aspiration if they are out of it, dehydration / shock if it has gone on long enough. Ask how much and how long. Salmonella: within 72 hours. Clean kitchen and proper cooking help. Nausea, vomit, belly pain, diarrhea. Toxins from food left out hit faster: about 2–3 hours to 8–12 hours. Same gut symptoms. Others named in the notes: E. coli, listeria, cyclospora (lettuce), norovirus — same flush-it-out picture. Botulism is the bad one. Classic: bad home-canned food. Swollen can = do not use. Also found in gas-station nacho cheese in an Arizona cluster. Botox is a tiny dose of the same toxin. Honey and infants: a baby’s gut is not ready; honey can carry spores. Paralysis starts at the top and works down. Kills when it hits the breathing muscles. Starts in the first 24 hours or as late as 4 days. Rare. No EMT antidote. ABCs. You may have to breathe for them with a BVM.

Plant poisoning: many house plants are poisonous if eaten. Kids eat them. Adults usually do not. Do not memorize plant names. Call Poison Control. Book answer: take the plant to the ER. A photo is fine in real life. ABCs and hospital.

Closing clues: opioids stay special because you have Narcan. Everything else: scene safe, ABCs, hospital. Stimulant stroke = more likely a bleed (high blood pressure). Cocaine can spasm heart vessels. Keep inhaled-solvent and severe-hypothermia patients calm. Irritable heart → VFib. Hydrogen sulfide = rotten eggs. Cyanide = almonds. Hot / blind / dry / red / mad = anticholinergic. SLUDGE / DUMBELS = cholinergic. Fluid from everywhere. DuoDote = atropine + 2-PAM. Not standard on every truck. Botulism = top-to-bottom paralysis. Bad canned food. Hits breathing muscles. Restrain only for active danger. Call the doctor if capacity is on the fence. Narcan is temporary. Push transport.
