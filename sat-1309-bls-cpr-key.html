BLS resuscitation and CPR

Key notes. Saturday 1:09. 12-question recap.

Infant, one rescuer: heel of one hand (new AHA). Two fingers were not getting depth. Infant or child, two rescuers: two-thumb encircling is preferred. Use the technique that actually hits depth and rate. For the test, default to those two.

Rate 100–120. Rotate compressors about every 2 minutes (5 cycles of 30:2 in adults; about 10 cycles of 15:2 in two-rescuer kids). Use the AED’s analyze pause as the swap. Breaths: 1 second, just to chest rise. Unresponsive with no gag: OPA.

Chest compression fraction is the percent of time you are actually compressing. Floor 60%. Target 80% or more. A 10% bump in CCF is about an 11% bump in survival. Do not pause more than 10 seconds.

Lucas and other mechanical CPR: consistent and they do not tire, but routine use is not recommended. Setup pauses wreck outcomes.

Do not start CPR for rigor, dependent lividity, decomposition, injuries not compatible with life, or a valid original DNR that is physically there (not a photo).

STOP: Starts breathing with a pulse. Transferred to equal or higher care. Out of strength (true exhaustion, no relief). Physician orders stop.

Respiratory arrest (pulse present): BVM, OPA, oxygen. Adult 1 breath every 6 seconds (10/min). Child and infant 1 breath every 2–3 seconds (20–30/min).

Mild choke: still coughing, still moving air. Hands off. Encourage cough. High-flow oxygen is fine. Severe adult or child: 5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts, repeat. Severe infant: 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts, head lower than body. Pregnant or very obese: 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts, not abdominal. If they go unresponsive: CPR 30:2, look in the mouth after compressions, pull only what you can see, no blind finger sweeps.

Opioid arrest: airway, breathing, circulation first. Naloxone after ABCs. They die of hypoxia.

Pregnant arrest: they have to be supine for compressions. If you have hands, push the uterus off to the side (usually maternal left). If you are alone, do the best CPR you can.

Advanced airway in: asynchronous CPR. Nonstop compressions at 100–120. 1 breath every 6 seconds. No more 30:2 pauses except analysis and shock.

Practice

  • Do this: show one-hand heel compressions on an infant manikin (or a folded towel). You did it right if the heel is on the sternum, not two fingers.
  • Do this: show two-thumb encircling with a partner. You did it right if thumbs compress and fingers wrap the chest.
  • Do this: swap compressors on an AED “analyzing” prompt. You did it right if the new compressor starts right after the shock or no-shock.
  • Do this: time a pause. You did it right if it stays under 10 seconds.
  • Do this: run mild vs severe choke. You did it right if a coughing patient gets encouragement, not thrusts.
  • Do this: 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts on an infant drill, head downhill. You did it right if you never used abdominal thrusts on the infant.
  • Do this: after 30 compressions on an unresponsive choke, look in the mouth. You did it right if you only pull what you can see.
  • Do this: say STOP out loud. You did it right if the four letters are Starts, Transferred, Out of strength, Physician.
