Soft tissue part 1 — key

All bleeding control starts with direct pressure. Direct pressure works → put on a pressure dressing so you can use your hand again. That is not a tourniquet. It does not cut off all flow. Arm or leg and pressure does not stop it → go straight to a tourniquet. Do not keep stacking gauze. Bleed in the groin or just inside the shoulder → you cannot get a tourniquet above it. Pack the wound with clotting gauze. Tourniquets and wound packing hurt a lot. Do them anyway. Lost red cells cannot carry oxygen. Blood at the hospital takes time.

Book tourniquet spot: a couple inches closer to the body than the hole (proximal). High and tight (all the way up the limb) is also okay. Most common here: CAT (combat application tourniquet). CAT steps: Velcro the strap down tight first. That alone already slows a lot of flow. Twist the stick until the bleeding STOPS — not until it “feels tight.” Lock the stick in the C-clamp. Write the time on the white strap. A loose gap between the band and the arm cannot be fixed by twisting. No blood and no oxygen reach that tissue. Time matters.

Skill station (~60 seconds): body substance isolation (BSI) and scene safety → major arm bleed → life threat → direct pressure → still bleeds → tourniquet on → bleed stopped and shock → high-flow oxygen, lay them down (supine), keep warm, rapid transport. Elastic / stretchy tourniquet wraps tighter and conforms better — better for tiny kid arms. The CAT has a stiff plastic piece that may not wrap a tiny arm. Junctional tourniquet: more military / tactical; not common on local EMS trucks. For groin or just inside the shoulder. Belt with an inflatable pad you pump like a blood pressure cuff. The pad pushes down into the hole.

Wound packing: clotting powder or, more often, gauze soaked with a clotting chemical (Celox-type). Shove it down to where the blood is coming from. Then hold hard pressure. First pack about 80% effective. If it soaks through, pull it and pack a fresh one → about 90%. Use packing for groin / inside-shoulder holes. Arms and legs: use a tourniquet. If they fight you because it hurts, stopping the bleed still comes first.

Arm crush: a tourniquet can trap junk from spreading. Belly crush: no tourniquet option. When the weight comes off, the next few minutes can go bad. Call medical control. Get them to the hospital.

Every open wound is dirty. Do not pick rocks or dirt out. Leave cleanup to the hospital. Dirt is not actively making it worse right now. Chemical in the wound is still burning → wash that now.

Evisceration: organs sticking out (often intestines). Do not push them back in. Moist sterile dressing right on the organs, then plastic / occlusive over that. Make a moist dressing by pouring sterile water or saline on regular sterile gauze. Anything that should be inside and is now outside gets a moist dressing — eyeballs too.

Impaled objects: leave it in. Pack gauze around it so it does not wiggle. Take it out only if it is in the cheek or mouth AND it blocks the airway you need to manage, or it is in the chest or back AND you need to do CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation). Breathing is fine → cheek/mouth object can stay.

Penetrating neck: major bleed → direct pressure on that side only. Never wrap all the way around the neck. Never squeeze both sides (brain passes out). Gauze on the wound, wrap under the opposite armpit. Air into a vessel → occlusive dressing to keep air out. No tourniquet on the neck.

Bites (animal and human): filthy. High infection. EMT care is a dry sterile dressing only. Hospital may clean, give antibiotics, tetanus, or operate. Rabies shots are a hospital / public-health call. Human bite to a knuckle (punch to the mouth) can swell so tight that blood flow drops (like compartment syndrome). Still just cover it.

Sometimes you immobilize (splint) even if it is not broken — to slow blood flow to the area. Less motion → less extra blood → swelling builds slower.

Open / sucking chest wound gets an occlusive (plastic) dressing. Tape 3 sides so extra air can vent out. Which side you leave open does not really matter. Burns start after this half.

Practice

Do this: start every bleed with direct pressure. You did it right if pressure that works got a pressure dressing, not a tourniquet.

Do this: arm or leg still bleeding after pressure → go straight to a tourniquet. You did it right if you did not stack more gauze.

Do this: groin or just-inside-shoulder bleed → pack with clotting gauze. You did it right if you said you cannot get a tourniquet above it.

Do this: put a CAT on — Velcro tight first, twist until bleeding STOPS, lock the stick in the C-clamp, write the time. You did it right if “feels tight” was never the stop point and a loose gap was not “fixed by twisting.”

Do this: run the ~60-second skill: BSI/scene → major arm bleed → direct pressure → still bleeds → TQ → bleed stopped + shock → high-flow oxygen, supine, warm, rapid transport. You did it right if shock care came after the bleed was stopped.

Do this: pick an elastic tourniquet for a tiny kid arm. You did it right if the CAT’s stiff plastic was why it may not wrap.

Do this: shove Celox-type gauze to the source, then hard pressure. You did it right if first pack ~80% and pull+repack ~90%, and arms/legs still got a tourniquet not packing.

Do this: leave dirt; wash chemical that is still burning. You did it right if hospital cleans the wound and you washed the chemical now.

Do this: evisceration — moist sterile on organs, then occlusive over; do not push guts back in. You did it right if sterile water or saline made the moist layer and eyeballs got moist too.

Do this: leave an impaled object and pack around it. You did it right if you removed it only for cheek/mouth blocking the airway you need, or chest/back for CPR.

Do this: neck bleed — one-side pressure, gauze under the opposite armpit; occlusive if air into a vessel. You did it right if you never wrapped around or squeezed both sides and never put a tourniquet on the neck.

Do this: cover a bite with a dry sterile dressing only. You did it right if antibiotics, tetanus, and rabies stayed hospital jobs.
