Chapter 1 — EMS systems

Safety NREMT

Always make sure you are safe before you answer the call or walk to the patient.

How EMS grew NREMT

Class walked the history. In 1966 a White Paper said trauma deaths were too high and that shove started modern EMS. In 1970 NHTSA (National Highway Traffic Safety Administration) was created under the Department of Transportation. In 1971 AAOS (American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons) published the first EMT textbook. The job moved from just a ride to a healthcare system with a physician watching over it.

Who does what NREMT

Four levels showed up. EMR (Emergency Medical Responder) is about 50 hours of CPR and first aid. EMT is about 130–150 hours of BLS (basic life support) and a limited med list. AEMT (Advanced EMT) adds IVs and more meds. Paramedic is ALS (advanced life support), at least 1,000 hours, with advanced airways, EKG work, and about 55–60 meds. Every EMT and paramedic works under the Medical Director’s license. That doctor is legally responsible for the care you give. Arizona historically recognized EMT and Paramedic only. EMR and AEMT arrived later and jobs are still limited. In the city, advanced skills usually stay with paramedics. Some rural agencies let trained EMTs start IVs if the medical director approves.

Orders and errors NREMT

Offline medical direction means standing orders. You do not call. Examples he named: CPR and AED, oxygen, epinephrine for anaphylaxis. That is most routine care. Online means you call a doctor by radio or phone. That order is for that one situation. New problem, new call. Use it when you are unsure, the case is odd, or you need extra permission. Three error types: a skills miss is knowing the right move and doing it wrong (oxygen at 2 LPM instead of 15). A rules miss is applying a known rule to the wrong problem (treating a stroke like low sugar). A knowledge miss is a new, incomplete picture and a wrong plan (wrong antidote).

Prevention, advocacy, consent NREMT

Primary prevention stops trouble before it starts: vaccines, seatbelts, public teaching. Secondary finds it early: screenings and early treatment of high blood pressure or cholesterol. Both sit on research. Advocacy means acting in the patient’s best interest, especially if they cannot speak. Speak up if care is rough or wrong. Honor wishes including a DNR (do not resuscitate). Explain what you are doing. Protect privacy and dignity. Report unsafe care or errors. If the person has decision-making capacity they can refuse treatment and transport even with a badly injured, partly amputated leg. Assess capacity carefully and write it all down. If they later become unconscious or lose capacity, implied consent takes over and you treat and transport.

Practice

  • Do this: Stand at the door and say out loud when you are safe enough to walk in. You did it right if you said safety first, before any patient care.
  • Do this: Recite the four levels with hours he gave: EMR, EMT, AEMT, paramedic. You did it right if EMR is about 50 hours, EMT 130–150, paramedic at least 1,000, and AEMT is IVs plus more meds.
  • Do this: Point at an imaginary radio. Say one offline example and one online example. You did it right if offline is CPR, oxygen, or epi without a call, and online is one situation by phone.
  • Do this: Act the three error types with the oxygen and stroke stories. You did it right if skills is 2 LPM instead of 15, rules is stroke treated as low sugar, knowledge is a wrong new plan.
  • Do this: Tell a partner they can refuse a ride with a bad leg if they have capacity. You did it right if you also said write it down, and if they pass out later you treat under implied consent.
  • Do this: Name one primary and one secondary prevention from class. You did it right if primary is vaccines, seatbelts, or teaching, and secondary is screening or early blood-pressure care.

Chapter 2 — Workplace safety

You first NREMT

Protect yourself so you can help. Scene, PPE (personal protective equipment), BSI (body substance isolation — gloves and eye protection), safe lifting, and awareness. You cannot help if you become the patient. At the front door look, listen, smell, and read the mood. Keep scanning the whole scene, not just the patient. After a task, pause and look again. Trust your gut. Call police for violence, weapons, threats, crime scenes, aggressive people, or suspected abuse. Call hazmat for spills, unknown stuff, chemical smells, toxic exposure, or industrial chemicals.

How stress looks NREMT

Fight-or-flight is the sympathetic system plus stress hormones. Heart and blood pressure climb, breathing speeds, pupils open, blood leaves the gut and skin for big muscles, heart, lungs, and brain, digestion slows, muscles tense, you sweat, pain dulls, senses sharpen. That package can look like shock: pale, cool, sweaty, nauseated, anxious. Your own stress can wreck decisions. Acute stress is during or right after a bad call. Delayed shows days to months later as nightmares, flashbacks, numbness. Cumulative is burnout from too many hard calls.

Infection control NREMT

A pathogen causes disease. Contamination is the germ on a person or surface. Exposure is the germ getting into you in a way that can infect. Transmission is how it moves: direct, indirect, droplet, airborne (TB and measles), or vector (bugs). Treat every patient as infectious. Hands and gloves every time. Add eyes, mask, gown, N95 as the risk says. Sanitizer at least 60% if hands are not dirty. Soap and water if soiled or after norovirus or C. diff. Don: hands, gown, mask, eyes, gloves. Doff: gloves, gown, eyes, mask, hands. N95 must be fit-tested. Never recap needles. Match other providers’ PPE. After a stick or splash: hand off the patient, wash or flush, do not squeeze or bleach, report now, get evaluated within hours, PEP if indicated, no cost to you.

Parking and talk NREMT

Order is you with gloves, then crew, then the patient. Park at least 100 feet from fire, crash, or hazmat. First unit parks before the wreck (upstream). Later units park past it (downstream). Hazmat: uphill and upwind. Test line: use the ERG (Emergency Response Guidebook). If it is not safe, stay out. With a critical patient, introduce yourself, explain, orient them, be honest without being cruel, leave hope without false hope, and notify family when you can. Grief stages are denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance, and people skip around.

Practice

  • Do this: At a closed door, look, listen, sniff, and say the mood out loud. You did it right if you named look, listen, smell, and mood before you entered.
  • Do this: Put on imaginary PPE in order, then take it off in order. You did it right if on is hands-gown-mask-eyes-gloves and off is gloves-gown-eyes-mask-hands.
  • Do this: Mime a needlestick: hand off, wash, do not squeeze, report. You did it right if you never squeezed or bleached and you said report now.
  • Do this: Park two chairs 100 feet from a wreck. First chair before, second past. You did it right if first is upstream and second is downstream.
  • Do this: Tell a partner they look pale and sweaty and name fight-or-flight vs shock as a look-alike. You did it right if you said stress can copy shock.
  • Do this: Introduce yourself to a stuffed animal as a critical patient. You did it right if you used your name, said you are an EMT, and oriented them without false hope.
  • Do this: Name the five grief stages out of order on purpose. You did it right if you said people skip around and still listed all five.

Chapter 3 — Medical, legal, ethical

Duty NREMT

Act in good faith and meet the standard of care — what a reasonably careful EMT with similar training would do in the same situation. Negligence is missing that standard and causing harm. All four pieces must be there: duty, breach, damages, causation. Abandonment is walking away without consent and without handing off to equal or higher care while they still need you. Stay in protocols, stay until a real handoff, write refusals, never leave someone who still needs care.

Consent NREMT

Expressed consent is an alert person with capacity who agrees, words or a nod or an arm held out, and they know the plan. Informed consent is the full version: nature, risks, benefits, alternatives. All informed consent is expressed. Not all expressed consent is fully informed. Implied is for the unresponsive or badly altered true emergency — a reasonable person would want help. Involuntary is a legal hold or danger to self or others. If they have capacity they can refuse even if they may die. If they lack capacity and it is an emergency, treat. A valid refusal needs capacity, a real explanation, a free choice, and a signature when you can get one. Capacity means they understand, they see what can happen, they can reason, and they can say a choice. Before you leave, reassess, confirm, tell them to call 911 if worse, try to leave them with a responsible person, get a signature or write that they refused to sign, and call medical control if your shop requires it.

Paper, privacy, restraint, death NREMT

A living will often is not enough for EMS to stop resuscitation. You need a valid DNR (do not resuscitate) or POLST. If they are awake and have capacity, that beats paper. If you doubt the paper, treat and call medical control. Share identifying care facts only with people who need them for care, when the law says so, with a court order, or with written permission. Not the news. Not social. Restraint is last resort: danger, protect, allow care, lesser steps failed. Never face-down. Never hog-tie. Soft restraints are the EMS pick. Chemical sedation is ALS. Watch breathing and circulation. Presumptive signs (unresponsive, no breath, no pulse, fixed pupils) do not let you withhold. Definitive signs are lividity, rigor, decomposition, injuries incompatible with life, or a valid DNR/POLST. Doubt means CPR. Medical examiner cases: unexpected, trauma, violence, suicide, suspicious, no recent physician. Police first. Do not move the body.

Practice

  • Do this: Recite the four negligence pieces on your fingers. You did it right if you said duty, breach, damages, causation.
  • Do this: Role-play a refusal: explain risks, ask them to sign, then they will not. You did it right if you reassessed, encouraged 911, and said you would write that they would not sign.
  • Do this: Hold out your arm as expressed consent, then close your eyes as implied. You did it right if expressed is an alert yes and implied is unresponsive emergency.
  • Do this: Say out loud when you would restrain and how you would never position them. You did it right if last resort, never prone, never hog-tie, watch ABCs.
  • Do this: Name presumptive vs definitive death and what you do if unsure. You did it right if doubt equals CPR and definitive includes lividity, rigor, decomposition, unsurvivable injury, or valid DNR/POLST.
  • Do this: Tell a friend you will not post the call on social media. You did it right if you said care team, law, court, or written permission only.

Chapter 4 — Communication

How you talk NREMT

Build trust. Start open (What happened?) then close in for facts. Eye contact, clear speech, real listening. Change for age, culture, hearing, vision, or fear. Honest without harsh. Confrontation calmly points at a mismatch (You said you do not drink, but I smell alcohol). Interpretation restates the fear. Explanation says what you are doing. Summary checks the story. Do not give false comfort, lead the answer, give life advice, cut them off, use jargon, judge, or talk over them with your partner. Kids: their words, caregivers in, simple language. Ten golden rules: keep eye contact, exchange names, tell the truth, use plain words, watch what you say about them, watch your body, speak slow and clear, face people who read lips, give time, stay calm and sure.

Radio, PCR, handoff NREMT

Radio is brief, professional, plain English, no name on the air, orders repeated back. Hospital notice uses SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation and ETA. The PCR (patient care report) is a legal permanent record. Put in who they are, the complaint, history, vitals, what you found, what you did, how they answered, and times. Facts only. If it is not on the page it did not happen. Cross one line through a mistake, initial, date, write the fix. Never erase. On a refusal write capacity, risks explained, that they understood, and the signature or that they would not sign. Care transfers only after a verbal handoff to equal or higher level and a completed PCR.

Practice

  • Do this: Ask a person What’s going on today? then one yes/no. You did it right if open came first.
  • Do this: Recite SBAR to an imaginary nurse while looking at your watch for ETA. You did it right if you hit Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation, ETA.
  • Do this: Say a radio report with no name and repeat a fake order back. You did it right if the name never appeared and you echoed the order.
  • Do this: Draw a one-line PCR fix on scrap paper, initial and date it. You did it right if you did not scribble it out or erase.
  • Do this: Walk the ten golden rules on your fingers. You did it right if you hit eye contact, names, truth, plain words, careful talk, body language, slow speech, face hearing-impaired, time, calm.
  • Do this: Hand a notebook to a partner and say you are transferring care. You did it right if you gave a verbal report and said the PCR is done.

Chapter 5 — Terms

Building words NREMT

A medical word is a prefix, a root, often an o just to say it, and a suffix. He listed tachy (fast), brady (slow), hyper (high), hypo (low), a/an (without), dys (hard, painful, or abnormal), pre and post. Roots: cardi heart, hepat liver, nephr kidney, neur nerve, psych mind, thorac chest. Suffixes: itis, pathy, algia, ectomy, megaly, ology, osis, emia, pnea, phagia. Numbers and colors as in the key. Practice builds only from that list: tachycardia is fast heart, bradypnea is slow breathing, hepatitis is liver inflammation, hypoglycemia is low sugar in the blood, bilateral is two sides, cyanosis is a blue abnormal look.

Map of the body NREMT

Left and right are the patient’s. Superior toward the head, inferior toward the feet, anterior/ventral front, posterior/dorsal back, medial in, lateral out, proximal closer to the trunk, distal farther, superficial near the skin, deep farther in. Stand them in anatomical position in your head: face you, arms at sides, palms forward. Movement words: flex, extend, abduct, adduct, rotate, circumduct, palm up or down, toes down or up. Positions you write: supine, prone, recovery on the side, Fowler about 45–90, semi-Fowler about 30–45, Trendelenburg rare (feet up), shock position older and use caution. Chart how you found them and how you transported.

Chapter 6 — Body (skeleton)

Map first NREMT

Anatomy is what it looks like. Physiology is how it works. Topographic anatomy is the surface landmarks you use to find deeper stuff. Always start from anatomical position: standing, facing you, arms at sides, palms forward. Sagittal cuts left and right (midsagittal is equal halves). Coronal or frontal cuts front and back. Transverse, horizontal, or axial cuts top and bottom — a cross-section. Life stacks: cells, tissues, organs, systems, the whole person.

Why you do ABC NREMT

Cells need oxygen, glucose, water and nutrients. Oxygen plus sugar makes energy (ATP), carbon dioxide, water, and heat. Brain cells die fast without oxygen or sugar. Skin lasts longer. That is why airway, breathing, and circulation come first.

Skeleton only NREMT

This chapter stopped at bone. The skeleton gives shape and support, protects organs, moves with muscle, stores calcium, and makes blood in marrow. An adult has 206 bones. The spine is 33 vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused in the sacrum, 4 fused in the coccyx. The cranium he listed was frontal, two parietal, and occipital. The face has 14 bones; he named nasal, zygoma, maxilla, and mandible. Twelve pairs of ribs and one sternum. Shoulder is clavicle and scapula. Arm is humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges. Pelvis is ilium, ischium, pubis on each side and it can bleed the most. Leg is femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges.

Saturday morning — Circulation and airway

Pump, pipes, fluid NREMT

Saturday morning was circulation, then airway. Three parts must work: pump, pipes, fluid. Fail one and you lose blood pressure and delivery of oxygen and sugar. Two loops: pulmonary to the lungs, systemic to the body. The heart is cardiac muscle and makes its own electricity, so unconscious does not mean the heart stopped. Blood comes deoxygenated into the right atrium, right ventricle, lungs, then oxygenated left atrium, left ventricle, body. Left wall is thicker. Weak left backs up into the lungs. Valves in order: tricuspid, pulmonic, mitral (bicuspid), aortic. Heart sounds are valves slamming. SA node in the right atrium is the primary pacemaker. AV node waits a beat so the ventricles fill. Arteries go away. Pulmonary arteries and fetal umbilical arteries are the deoxygenated exceptions. Coronaries off the aorta feed the heart muscle. Chamber blood does not.

Numbers and bleeds NREMT

Resting adult rate 60–100, average 70. Fast is tachycardia, slow is bradycardia. Athletes can live in the 40s. Stroke volume is one squeeze. Cardiac output is that times rate. Example 80 mL times 70 is 5.6 liters a minute, about the whole tank. You cannot measure stroke volume on scene so you use blood pressure, skin, and how they think. Preload is return fill. Bleed the floor and preload drops and they can faint. Brain wants oxygen, sugar, and temperature. Afterload is how tight the pipes are. Squeeze raises pressure. Open pipes drop it. Anaphylaxis opens them. Epi’s job he named here is a hard squeeze. Arterial blood is bright and spurts. Venous is darker and oozes. Pulse sites he named: neck, wrist, groin, brachial on the humerus, top of the foot, behind the ankle bump. Three reasons: dead or not, rate, and past an injury.

Belly and airway NREMT

Solid organs bleed. Hollow organs spill infection. Quadrants cross at the navel. Spleen is left upper and handles old red cells, about 120 days. Liver detoxes and can bleed a lot. Scene still first. Adult: patient first. Child: parent first if present. Airway that morning: tongue is the usual block. Medical open is head-tilt chin-lift. Trauma is jaw thrust. OPA if no gag. NPA if gag, measure nose to earlobe. SpO2 at least 94%. Cannula 2–6, non-rebreather 10–15, bag at 15 if they cannot breathe enough. Suction only while coming out; that morning the clock was 15/10/5. Unconscious and breathing goes on the side. Distress still moves air. Arrest does not.

Saturday — Endocrine, gut, nerves

Epi package NREMT

Afternoon was glands, nerves, then gut. Adrenal glands sit on the kidneys. Adrenaline is epinephrine. Alpha receptors squeeze vessels. Beta-1 is the heart, faster and harder. Beta-2 is the lungs, tubes open. Epi binds both families. Albuterol is a beta-2 drug. Beta blockers sit on beta-1 and block it. Baroreceptors live in two spots only: the aorta and the carotids. Chemoreceptors taste oxygen, carbon dioxide, sugar, and pH. The fight-or-flight look is non-specific — bleed, weak pump, or wide-open anaphylaxis all fire the same package.

Brain and cord NREMT

Sympathetic steals blood from gut and sex to survive now. Parasympathetic is rest, digest, feed and breed. Central nervous system is brain and cord. Peripheral is everything else. Motor goes out, sensory comes in. Somatic is you moving on purpose. Autonomic is automatic. Brainstem (leave midbrain, pons, medulla lumped) runs blood pressure and breathing. Cerebrum is personality, seizures, strokes. Cerebellum is coordination. Broca’s in left frontal makes speech. Occipital sees. Temporal understands words. You recognize a stroke. You do not treat until a CT. Hypothalamus is temperature. RAS is the wake gate. CSF cushions. Fat neck veins mean backup. Flat veins mean empty. Phrenic nerves C3, 4, and 5 keep the diaphragm alive.

Gut and skin NREMT

Homeostasis numbers he said: heart 60–100, breaths 12–20, sugar 80–120, SpO2 94–99. Burns: first superficial, second into dermis, third through all layers. Small bowel absorbs. Peristalsis is waves. Appendix is hollow off the large bowel. Pain: onset, better or worse, their word, where, radiation, patient’s sides. Pancreas sits mid-upper and can hurt the back. Insulin drops sugar. Glucagon raises it. Liver is solid in the right upper: detox, bile, clotting factors. That is where the 10:48 talk stopped.

Saturday 11:27 review

The review hour NREMT

This was a recap of gut, glands, and shock. Clotting needs about a dozen factors. Miss one and there is no clot. Hemophilia patients do not clot. Pressure holds; you let go and they bleed again. Treatment is giving the missing factors. Liver makes bile for fat, stores extra sugar as glycogen, and can pull it back in an emergency, or extra sugar leaves in urine. Gallbladder is the green storage sac. Remove it and they still make bile but cannot store it, so fatty meals sit badly. Small bowel meets large bowel in the right lower quadrant. The appendix hangs off the start of the large bowel, so appendicitis hurts right lower. Large bowel climbs the right, crosses, drops the left, and exits midline. It pulls water. No large bowel means diarrhea.

Lymph to shock NREMT

Lymph is a sewer with no heart. Movement and breathing push it. Plague was the only disease name he tossed. Hypothalamus bridges electricity to hormone and talks to the pituitary, the master gland. The two hormones you will live with are epinephrine and insulin. Insulin lets cells use glucose. Type 1 means the pancreas stops making it. Thyroid is metabolism. Parathyroid is calcium. Kidneys have three pipes: blood in, blood kept, urine out. They handle fluid, pH, and blood pressure by volume. Stones are usually calcium. After you fix life threats, check hands, feet, face, genitals. Cells burn oxygen and sugar into energy, carbon dioxide, water, and heat (98.6). Shock is hypoperfusion. They feel cold in a warm room. Blankets no matter why. Without oxygen they make lactic acid. That is anaerobic.

Chapter 8 — Moving patients

Gear NREMT

Afternoon was how you move people. Learn when each tool is right, not every brand name. The gurney is every transport. It is not for stairs or dirt. Head first because the head is heavier. Lock it to the floor. Everybody rides on it, not a bench if you can help it. Powered stretchers weigh 40–145 pounds empty. Two hands, front and back. Take a curb straight. A backboard is a full-body splint. Strap chest, hips, and legs. Use it for neck or back pain, numb or weak or paralyzed, unconscious trauma, or someone who cannot tell you the story. If they are too wide, the board can make things worse — justify it or call medical control. Log roll needs four, head runs the count, collar first, never lift by the head. Legs do the lift. Palms up about ten inches. Lift assist is more humans, not a second truck. His shop used about 250 pounds as a two-person limit. Bariatric trouble is width. Keep the load low. Use a lift-gate or a ramp and a winch. Diamond carry is four sides. Stair chair is for the awake person with no spine worry when there is no elevator. Unconscious on stairs goes on a board, feet first down.

When to rush and how to lie NREMT

Emergency move is seconds: fire, pulseless in a wreck, CPR. You may skip spine. Urgent is about a minute and the board will still be incomplete. Non-urgent is stable and you lift the least. Vest devices are rare sitting-car tools for the stable awake with a collar. Vacuum mattress for a very bent spine. Stokes for water and rope. Scoop for a hip. Isolette is the newborn box and usually not your patient. No medical reason means let them pick a comfortable position. Shock is lots of oxygen, warm, flat, leave now — not feet in the air. Pregnant past twenty weeks lies on the left so the uterus does not crush the big vessels. Unresponsive and breathing without a spine worry goes on the side. Hard breathing sits up. Restraints: five people, on the back, after you thought about oxygen, sugar, and head, and you write it. Gloves every time. Ask if the crew is strong enough. Stronger person takes the head.

Chapter 9 — Patient assessment

The flow NREMT

Chapter 9 is the whole job in five pieces: scene, primary, history, secondary, reassess. Primary is the biggest test slice, about 35–45 percent. It starts when you get eyes on. Seven steps: impression, how awake, airway, breathing, circulation, a fast trauma scan, then go or stay. Rule: find a life threat, treat that life threat. First impression question is could they be dead. If maybe, find a pulse and start CPR. A mute stroke can fool you. Bright spurting blood is now, not later on the sheet.

Safety and AVPU NREMT

His safety order is you, crew, bystanders, patient. BSI then scene. Skip either and the skill sheet dies. Park 100 feet. First truck makes a wall before the wreck. Second parks past it. MOI is the crash story. NOI is the illness. Many people with the same complaint is hazmat — stay out. More than one patient is an MCI, but this block is one patient. Six lines every time: BSI, safety, MOI or NOI, count, extra help, think C-spine. AVPU: A is looking around. V answers your hello. P only if they need pain. U is nothing. Then A&O times 4 if they are A. Miss a piece and you lean implied consent unless the miss is just I am not from here.

Airway through priority NREMT

Talking or crying means the airway is good. Unconscious means assume the tongue. Open medical with a head tilt. Open trauma with a jaw thrust. Four steps: open, assess, clear, maintain. Gurgling is suction. Adult slow under 8 almost always needs a bag. Fast over 28 is a maybe. Pulse: wrist if awake, neck if an adult or child is out, brachial if an infant is out. Cap refill under two seconds is good and matters on kids. Pressure first on a bleed. Tourniquet toward the body on a limb that will not stop. Never on a neck. Sixty seconds of trauma-naked only on critical trauma. Priority is high or low. High rides now. Golden hour is injury to the operating room. Platinum 10 is your on-scene clock.

History and Sunday exam NREMT

History is OPQRST for today and SAMPLE for the wider past. Open questions first. No historians means skip to the exam. Sunday added how you ask about drugs (twice, then drop it), how you report abuse without accusing, and pregnancy questions on lower belly pain. Secondary is look, listen, feel. Focused if they can point to one part. Full head-to-toe if they are out or the mechanism is big. DCAP-BTLS is the injury alphabet. Breath sounds split upper (you hear them in the room) and lower (stethoscope). First blood pressure is manual. Cuff size lies if wrong. GCS example he drilled: eyes to sound, confused, localizes is 12. Sugar 80–120. Pulse ox 94–99 and carbon monoxide can fake 100. After the impression come meds, but oxygen and breathing still go first. Reassess every five if they are crashing, every fifteen if they are stable. A huge change sends you back to primary. On the test, call for permission before a drug unless the stem already gave the order.

Practice

  • Do this: Say the six scene lines while putting on gloves. You did it right if BSI and safety were first and C-spine was last of the six.
  • Do this: Walk AVPU on a willing person. Stop at V. Do not cause pain. You did it right if you never did a sternal rub on an answering person.
  • Do this: Ask A&O times 4: name, where, year, president. You did it right if a miss made you say implied consent unless the miss was circumstantial.
  • Do this: Show jaw thrust vs head tilt on a pillow. You did it right if trauma did not tilt the neck.
  • Do this: Press a fingernail for cap refill and say under two vs over two. You did it right if over two was delayed and a kid shock clue.
  • Do this: Mime direct pressure, then a proximal tourniquet on a thigh. You did it right if neck was never an option.
  • Do this: Recite OPQRST then SAMPLE out loud without notes. You did it right if Quality was their word and Time was when it started.
  • Do this: Time a 60-second rapid scan on a clothed partner without stripping them. You did it right if you said trauma-naked is only for critical trauma.
  • Do this: Score GCS: eyes to sound, confused, localizes. You did it right if you said 3+4+5=12.
  • Do this: Set a phone timer and say critical reassess every 5, stable every 15. You did it right if a fake collapse sent you back to primary.
  • Do this: Ask drugs twice the way he said, then chart denies. You did it right if the second ask was no judgment and you dropped it.
  • Do this: Size an imaginary cuff on an upper arm (about two-thirds). You did it right if too big meant a falsely low number.

Airway and oxygen

How air works NREMT

Sunday started with why breathing matters. After you are safe and they are alive, airway is first unless a huge bleed is already spraying in your face. Upper airway sits above the voice box and warms, filters, and humidifies. The epiglottis is the lid. Lower airway is the gas-exchange tree down to grape-sac alveoli. The phrenic nerve at C3, 4, and 5 runs the diaphragm. High neck injuries can steal the breath. Ventilation is the bellows. Respiration is the swap. Oxygenation is seats on hemoglobin. Then the pump. Inhale makes a vacuum. We cannot, so we push, and some air can hit the stomach. Watch chest rise for volume. Bag only until it rises. The brain mostly drives on high carbon dioxide. Adequate adult air is 12–20 with real rise and quiet equal lungs — they can still look blue. Inadequate means you take over. Pattern names: agonal with CPR, Cheyne–Stokes with a wave and a pause, ataxic with no pattern, Kussmaul deep and fast for acid — do not crush that on purpose. Oxygen if the number is under 94, they say they cannot breathe, or they look like shock. The probe can take a minute and it can lie.

Open and suction NREMT

Talking or a screaming kid means the path is open. Unconscious means the tongue. You work from the crown with them on their back. Head tilt only when the story has no spine. Unknown means jaw thrust and you are stuck holding it. Look and listen before you count breaths. Gurgling or visible junk is suction. If the machine is not up, roll them and use gravity. Aspiration is ugly math on top of the first problem. Rigid tube for the mouth. Measure like an oral airway. Suction off on the way in, circles out, until clear up to ten seconds for every age. Then give them oxygen. If they are still dirty, bag two minutes and suction ten again. Pushing a little junk is bad. No air at all is death. Sunday retired the old 15/10/5 clock.

Tubes, tanks, masks, choke NREMT

Maintain means keep the tongue up when you let go. Oral airway if they are fully out and have no gag. Measure mouth to ear or jaw. Twist 180 or 90. Gag means out and get ready to suction. Nasal airway if they still gag and the face and head are not smashed. Bevel to the middle wall, flush to the nostril. Head trauma plus a gag means hold the jaw and wait for ALS. Tanks stay in a hand or on their side. D is portable, H is the house tank, about 2,000 psi full. Any face mask is 10–15 liters. Non-rebreather is the test favorite for someone who still breathes enough. Cannula is 1–6. Bag is a tight seal and 100 percent. Adult every six seconds. Kids every two to three. Unconscious plus good rate and rise is not an automatic bag. CPAP is a tight blowing mask for lung water in a talking, sitting person with a blood pressure of at least 90. Trauma and vomit take it off the table. Mild choke: they still move air, hands off. Severe: five backs and five abdominals for adult and child, infant backs and chests, unresponsive goes to CPR.

Practice

  • Do this: Watch a chest and say ventilation, respiration, oxygenation, circulation in order. You did it right if good rise plus blue made you blame swap or pump, not the bellows.
  • Do this: Bag a pillow: squeeze only until it rises, then stop. You did it right if you did not chase half a bag.
  • Do this: Pick head tilt vs jaw thrust for bed vs sidewalk. You did it right if sidewalk was jaw thrust and you did not let go.
  • Do this: Measure a straw from mouth corner to earlobe, mime suction off in, circles out, ten seconds. You did it right if all ages were ten seconds and you said bag two minutes if still dirty.
  • Do this: Size an OPA on a manikin or your own face landmarks, then an NPA nose to ear. You did it right if OPA was no-gag only and NPA skipped any head or face trauma.
  • Do this: Say no OPA and no NPA for gag plus face trauma, hold the jaw. You did it right if you asked for a paramedic airway.
  • Do this: Lay a water bottle on its side and call it a tank that must not stand free. You did it right if you named missile and pin index oxygen-only.
  • Do this: Count adult bagging out loud: one every 6 seconds for a minute. You did it right if you landed about 10 breaths and each squeeze was about one second.
  • Do this: Recite CPAP yes (talking, sitting, fluid, pressure at least 90) and three nos. You did it right if vomit, trauma, or wanting to lie down were nos.
  • Do this: On a standing partner, mime 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts, then infant 5 and 5 chest on a doll. You did it right if mild choke stayed hands-off and unresponsive became CPR.
