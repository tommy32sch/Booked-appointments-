# Pulmonary embolism and obstructive shock — key

A pulmonary embolism (PE) blocks the blood-flow pipe to the lungs, not the airflow pipes. The airway can still be open. Air can still move.

Breath sounds tell you about air movement, not blood flow. In a PE you can hear clear, equal breath sounds, see good chest rise, and still watch them get more blue because gas exchange is failing.

Ventilation is moving air in and out. Respiration (gas exchange) is moving oxygen into blood and carbon dioxide out. In a PE, ventilation can look good while oxygenation is bad.

Classic PE pattern: sudden difficulty breathing, maybe chest pain, clear lungs, good chest rise, on high-flow oxygen (non-rebreather about 15 L/min), and the saturation keeps dropping with worsening cyanosis.

PE, tension pneumothorax, and cardiac tamponade are obstructive shock.

You cannot pull the clot or drain the sac. Treat for shock and get to the hospital fast. Paramedics may needle-decompress a tension pneumothorax. That is not an EMT skill on this recap.

Tension extras: severe dyspnea, absent or decreased sounds on one side, jugular vein distension (JVD), tracheal deviation late, hypotension.

Tamponade extras: JVD, hypotension, muffled heart sounds.

High-flow oxygen. Non-rebreather if breathing is adequate. Bag-valve mask if it is not. Keep them warm. Load and go. Alert the hospital to possible obstructive shock / PE. Reassess an unstable patient every 5 minutes.
