Abdominal injuries — detailed

Surgery is the answer. Know the quadrants. Do not wait for a fat, bruised belly.

Part 1 — Abdomen

Big rules

Pain after trauma = assume something is bleeding and something is leaking. Go.

Solid organs bleed (hemorrhagic shock). Hollow organs leak (infection later).

The belly hides a lot of blood. Bruising around the belly button is late.

Good lungs + shock you cannot explain = think abdomen.

Guts out: moist dressing on the organs, plastic over that for heat. Do not push them back in.

The belly hides blood

About 6 liters of blood in the body. The abdomen can hold a couple of 2-liter bottles before it looks swollen or bruised.

Waiting for a red, tight, hot belly means you are already late.

Right after the wreck you will not see fever even if a hollow organ leaked. Infection takes time.

Chest bleed changes breath sounds. Breath sounds are fine + shock = assume the bleed is in the abdomen.

Quadrants

AreaOrgans
Right upperLiver (solid) and gallbladder (hollow)
Left upperStomach (hollow) and spleen (solid)
Upper middle / both uppersPancreas. Also sits in the back.
Lower quadrantsSmall and large intestine. All hollow. Appendix = right lower.
Flank / backKidneys. Not a front-quadrant organ.

Solid vs hollow

TypeWhat happens
Solid (liver, spleen, kidney, pancreas)Bleed. Hemorrhagic shock.
Hollow (stomach, gallbladder, intestines, ureters, bladder)Spill acid, bile, stool, or urine. Irritates the lining (peritonitis). Infection / septic shock later.

Early leak and early bleed feel the same: pain, tenderness, tight muscles. You will not sort them on scene. Transport.

Mesentery = the fold that holds the intestines and carries their blood. It can bleed even if the liver and spleen are fine. Aorta and vena cava also run through the belly.

Kids vs adults

Adult organs sit farther apart and hide under a longer rib cage.

Kid organs are bigger for the belly, packed closer, and the short rib cage leaves them more exposed.

One hit on a kid can smash liver and spleen at the same time.

Organ clues

OrganWhat to remember
LiverBiggest bleeder. Right lower ribs can cut it. Pain can go to the right shoulder (same path as gallbladder).
SpleenLeft upper. Filters old red cells. Those cells still bleed.
KidneyBack compartment. Word = flank.
PancreasBook counts it as solid (bleeds more than it leaks). Pain can shoot to the back.
DiaphragmTear lets bowel into the chest. Hard to breathe. Gurgles when you listen on the chest.

Front cavity = liver, spleen, stomach, intestines. Back cavity (retroperitoneal) = kidneys, pancreas, aorta, vena cava. Back-cavity problems give back or flank pain.

Cullen’s sign: bruise around the belly button = a lot of blood already inside. Do not wait for it.

Seatbelt and pregnancy

The belt should catch the pelvis, not the soft belly.

Belt too high: the body flies forward and the guts get pinched between the belt and the spine.

Classic: kid who still needs a booster. The booster lifts them so the belt hits bone.

Pregnant belly: organs are pushed around and the baby is in the strike zone.

Second half of pregnancy: transport on the left side. Lying flat lets the uterus sandwich the aorta and vena cava against the spine and the blood pressure drops (supine hypotensive syndrome). First half is not big enough to do this.

Dress the wound

High belly that could be chest → plastic / occlusive, same as a chest hole.

Lower belly, no chest concern → dry sterile dressing.

You cannot tell how deep a stab is. Vitals tell you how scared to be.

Evisceration

Do not push organs back in.

Worries: bleeding, infection, drying out, heat loss.

  1. Moist sterile dressing on the organs.
  2. Plastic over that. Here the plastic is fake skin to hold heat, not an air seal.

No big plastic? Wet dressing, then bulky dry, then blankets.

Exam and history

XABCs first. Board after life threats. Platinum 10.

Look first. Push all four quadrants. Hurt quadrant LAST. Poke the sore spot first and the whole belly lights up.

Feel for tight, swollen, guarding (arms over the belly or they flex against your hand), and masses.

Pulsating mass = abdominal aortic aneurysm until proven otherwise. Not a normal pulse-check spot.

Ask about blood in vomit, stool, or urine only if there has been time. Four minutes after the crash they have not peed yet.

SAMPLE still matters on a wreck if you have time. Blood thinners = they bleed more. Daily blood-pressure pills block the fight-or-flight rise, so they crash through compensated shock faster.

A sore belly would rather not lie flat. Shock wins. If they are in shock they go supine.

Part 1 must-remember lineup

Right upper = liver and gallbladder. Left upper = stomach and spleen. Appendix = right lower. Kidney = flank.

Solid bleeds. Hollow leaks. No fever yet on a fresh trauma.

Do not wait for Cullen’s or a fat belly.

Good lungs + hidden shock = abdomen.

Bowel sounds in the chest = torn diaphragm.

Seatbelt on the pelvis. Booster for kids.

Second-half pregnancy = left side.

Guts out: moist then plastic. Palpate the sore quadrant last.

Shock goes flat. Blood-pressure pills and blood thinners matter.

Part 2 — Kidneys, pelvis, genitals

Big rules

Flank = kidney. Always.

Pelvis can dump a third of the blood tank and poke hollow organs at the same time.

Genitals only jump the line if they are pouring blood.

Pregnant trauma: treat shock early. Mom looks fine while the baby is already in trouble.

Cold patients cannot clot. Keep them warm.

Named signs

NameMeaning
CushingHigh pressure in the head. High and wide pulse pressure. Slow pulse.
BeckTamponade. Fat neck veins, muffled heart, narrow pulse pressure.
KehrPain to the shoulder. Class used Right upper → right shoulder (gallbladder / liver). Book classic is often left shoulder from a smashed spleen.
CullenBruise around the belly button. Blood in the front abdomen. Late.
Gray TurnerBruise on the flank. Blood in the back compartment. Late.

Kidney and bladder

Kidney: if they want this organ they will say flank. Bruising, tenderness, swelling on the flank. Blood in the urine if they have peed since the wreck.

Bladder: hollow. Urine spills into the pelvis. Infection later. Early = just pain.

A sudden stop can tear the urethra off the bladder. Same idea as the retina peeling or the aorta tearing off the heart: the insides keep going when the body slams stop.

Pelvis

Worst bone for bleeding. A bad break can dump 2+ liters. The body only has about 6. That is a third of the tank.

One smashed pelvis can make three life threats at once:

  • Bleed from the broken bone itself
  • Cut the femoral artery or vein next to it
  • Poke the bladder or rectum → infection later

Dressings and packing

Chest hole: always plastic.

High belly: lean plastic (the diaphragm moves).

Low belly: dry sterile.

Pack hemostatic gauze only at junctions — groin and just inside the shoulder. You cannot pack a deep belly or a chest. Surgery.

Impaled in the abdomen stays. It is not in the airway and it is not blocking CPR.

Guts out: moist on the organs, plastic over that for heat. Do not put them back.

Keep every trauma / shock patient warm. Cold blood does not clot. That is why trauma rooms feel like ovens.

Genitals

Not a life threat unless they are pouring blood. Painful and embarrassing is not the same as dying right now.

Any bleed anywhere gets a dry sterile dressing.

Vaginal bleed: pad on the outside. Do not pack the vagina. Save the pads. The hospital counts them.

Not bleeding: genitals get a moist sterile dressing (same short list as eyes and guts).

Blood → dry. Never says blood → moist.

Avulsed tissue: sterile wrap, plastic, keep cool, bring it.

Internal female organs are well protected until pregnancy. Then the uterus rises and you have two patients.

Pregnant trauma

Fight-or-flight shuts blood to the gut and uterus first. Mom saves herself. The fetus loses flow.

Pregnant blood volume is higher, so mom looks fine longer. When you finally see shock, the baby is already in trouble. Treat shock early.

Narcan: if mom used opioids the whole pregnancy, the fetus may be dependent. Naloxone hits both. Breathing is the only thing that will kill her in that minute — you can bag her. Call medical control. Do not decide Narcan yourself.

Privacy and assault

Move to the ambulance. Same-gender provider if they are stable and you can wait.

Life threat = no shopping for a female medic. Save them first.

Sexual assault: ABCs first, then kindness. You may be the first safe person they see.

Ask them not to pee, wash, or change if they can wait for a hospital exam.

Clothes and evidence go in a paper bag. Plastic traps moisture and ruins DNA.

Part 2 must-remember lineup

Flank = kidney. Gray Turner = flank bruise. Cullen = belly-button bruise.

Pelvis = 2+ liters and three life threats.

Cold = cannot clot.

Genital bleed = dry pad outside. No packing.

Pregnant + trauma = treat shock early.

Narcan in pregnancy = call the doctor. You can just breathe for her.
