# BLS resuscitation and CPR — detailed

This talk is BLS updates: infant technique, compression fraction, when not to start, STOP, choking, opioids, pregnancy, and asynchronous CPR.

Infant, one rescuer: heel of one hand. Two fingers were not deep enough. Two rescuers on an infant or child: two-thumb encircling stays preferred. One person thumbs, the other owns the airway. In the field, pick the grip that actually delivers depth and rate. On the test, those two defaults.

Keep 100–120. Swap about every 2 minutes because quality falls even if you feel fine. Adults: 5 cycles of 30:2. Two-rescuer kids: about 10 cycles of 15:2. The AED analyzes about every 2 minutes. Use that hands-off prompt to change compressors. Breaths are 1 second to visible rise. Put an OPA in an unresponsive patient with no gag. In arrest the tongue falls back, so the OPA is standard.

Chest compression fraction is how much of the clock is actual compressions. Under 60% survival is about zero. Aim for 80% or more. Keep pauses under 10 seconds. Plan the airway and devices around ongoing compressions. A 10% rise in CCF is about an 11% rise in survival.

A suction plunger and an impedance threshold device exist. Evidence is weak and they are uncommon. Lucas is a piston. A load-distributing band squeezes the chest. They do not fatigue and they stay consistent. AHA still does not want routine mechanical CPR: the setup pause is too long.

Do not start if they have rigor, dependent lividity, decomposition, or trauma not compatible with life, or if a valid original DNR is in your hand. A photo is not enough.

STOP means you can stop: they start breathing with a pulse, you transfer to equal or higher care, you are truly out of strength with nobody to relieve you, or a physician tells you to terminate. Some agencies stop after 8–15+ minutes of CPR with unknown downtime and persistent asystole or repeated no-shock. If the AED keeps finding shockable rhythms, most physicians keep going.

Pulse, no breathing: respiratory arrest. BVM, OPA, oxygen. Adult 1 every 6 seconds. Child and infant 1 every 2–3 seconds. Reassess pulse and breathing every 2 minutes. No pulse: cardiac arrest, compressions.

Mild choke: coughing, moving air. Do not thrust. Let them cough. Oxygen is fine. Severe adult or child: 5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts until it comes out or they go down. Severe infant: 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts, head lower than the body. Pregnant or very obese: chest thrusts instead of abdominal. Unresponsive: CPR 30:2. After compressions, look. If you can see it and grab it, take it. No blind sweeps. Two breaths. Repeat.

Opioid: they die of no oxygen, not the drug sitting there. Ventilate or do CPR first. Naloxone after ABCs.

Second-half pregnancy: the uterus squishes the aorta and IVC when they lie flat. They still need to be supine for compressions. If you have a spare pair of hands, push the uterus to the side, usually the maternal left. Alone, high-quality CPR beats a bad displacement plus bad compressions.

Once an advanced airway is in, drop 30:2. Nonstop compressions 100–120. One breath every 6 seconds. Air can only go to the lungs, so overlapping breaths are fine and CCF goes up. Pause only for analyze and shock, still under 10 seconds.

## Practice
Do this: count 5 cycles of 30:2 and call the swap. You did it right if that was about 2 minutes.

Do this: say CCF floor and target. You did it right if 60% is the floor and 80% is the goal.

Do this: refuse a photo DNR. You did it right if you said you need the original form in hand.

Do this: ventilate a child in respiratory arrest at 1 breath every 2–3 seconds. You did it right if you were near 20–30 a minute, not 10.

Do this: 5 and 5 on a pregnant choke drill with chest thrusts. You did it right if you never went abdominal.

Do this: name uterine displacement on a second-rescuer pregnant arrest. You did it right if the uterus went laterally and compressions never stopped.

Do this: switch to 1 breath every 6 seconds once a tube is in, with no compression pause. You did it right if compressions never cycled 30:2 after that.

Do this: put naloxone after the first BVM or the first compression cycle, not before. You did it right if ABCs led.
