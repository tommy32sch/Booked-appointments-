# Oxygen tank and BVM — key

Not a trauma chapter. These two stations are the first skills you practice. Say the step while your hands do it.

## Big rules

Say it while you do it.

Medical assessment fails if you never name the field impression. If you are lost, you skipped a check.

NRB is 10–15 L/min. Less than 10 is a straw. You suffocate them.

BVM: 1 breath every 6 seconds. Squeeze 1 second. Just until the chest rises.

Suction no more than 10 seconds. Pulse + breathing check no more than 10 seconds.

## What you test

- Trauma assessment — find life threats and fix them.
- Medical assessment — same walk-through, then you must say the field impression.
- Oxygen tank + non-rebreather (NRB).
- BVM on an adult who is not breathing.
- CPR / cardiac arrest.
- Bleeding control / tourniquet.
- Backboard.

The last five are “do the sheet in order.” Trauma and medical are “do the sheet AND apply it.” Review Chapter 10. That chapter is the skill sheet in book form.

Medical presentations will be classic. Crushing chest pressure is a heart attack.

If you finish medical and have no idea, you skipped a box. Look at your notes. Blood sugar 40. That was the problem.

## Oxygen tank + NRB

No big story. Build a tank. Put a mask on. About 60–90 seconds. Real life the tank is already built. Testing makes you build it now.

1. BSI. Scene safe.
2. Gather tank, regulator, mask, wrench.
3. Crack the tank. Quick open, then close. Blows dirt out of the outlet. Open = lefty-loosey.
4. Seat the regulator. Two pins go in the two holes first. Then screw it down. It should sit at a right angle. Tilted = not seated.
5. Open the tank one full turn. Gas is now in the regulator.
6. Check the gauge. Full tank is about 2000 PSI. Listen for a hiss. A leak is usually a dead brass washer.
7. Hook the mask tubing to the port.
8. Turn flow to 10–15 L/min from the start. Do not start low and forget.
9. Prefill the bag. Finger on the nose flap so gas can only go into the bag.
10. Put the mask on. Snug enough it does not fall off. Not a CPAP cinch.

If you are not holding the tank, lay it down. A standing tank with a snapped neck becomes a missile.

Shortcut: set 10–15 first. Then the “adjust to at least 10” line is already done.

0.5 L/min is not a treatment. NRB below 10 restricts breathing.

Talk track (say while you do it): “BSI, scene safe. Gathering equipment. Cracking the tank. Assembling the regulator. Opening the tank. Checking pressure. Listening for leaks. Attaching the mask. Fifteen liters. Prefilling the bag. Applying the mask.”

## BVM — adult not breathing

Follow the sheet. Mini-script:

1. BSI. Scene safe.
2. Patient looks bad. Verbal, then pain (sternal rub). No response.
3. Call for more EMS.
4. Check breathing and pulse together. No more than 10 seconds.
   The station says: unresponsive, not breathing, weak pulse of 60. Alive, not breathing. Keep going.
5. Open the airway. Head-tilt chin-lift is fine here. No trauma story.
6. Look in the mouth. You find: full of vomit. Suction.
   - Turn it on. Test that it sucks.
   - Measure corner of mouth to earlobe.
   - Insert with suction OFF.
   - Suction ON while you come out. Circle. No more than 10 seconds.
   The station says: mouth is now clear.
7. Re-open the airway. The tongue may have fallen back.
8. OPA. Measure corner of mouth to earlobe. Insert sideways. Twist 90°.
   The station says: no gag. Patient accepts it. Airway is open, checked, cleared, and held.
9. BVM on high-flow oxygen at 15 L/min. C-E grip. Seal the mask.
10. 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 per minute). Squeeze about 1 second. Stop when the chest rises.

Recheck the pulse. They could lose it next. AHA says every 2 minutes. Critical reassess is every 5. Either is fine.

Count out loud so your hands match your mouth.

The printed sheet may have you bag on room air first, then add oxygen after they say ventilations are working. Do whatever that day’s sheet says. In class, 15 L went on right away.

Talk track while you bag: “One breath every six seconds.” Then count it.

## Practice

Do this: build an O2 tank from empty through mask on, saying each step out loud. You did it right if you cracked, seated pins-first at a right angle, opened one full turn, checked ~2000 PSI / hiss, set 10–15 from the start, prefilled, and applied the mask.

Do this: set NRB flow. You did it right if you said 10–15 L/min and never left it below 10.

Do this: use the shortcut on the flow dial. You did it right if you set 10–15 first so “adjust to at least 10” is already done.

Do this: lay a tank down when you are not holding it. You did it right if a standing tank never sat alone with a regulator on.

Do this: run pulse + breathing together. You did it right if the check stayed ≤10 seconds.

Do this: suction a vomited mouth. You did it right if you tested suction, measured corner-of-mouth to earlobe, inserted OFF, suctioned ON coming out in a circle, and stopped by 10 seconds.

Do this: place an OPA. You did it right if you measured corner-of-mouth to earlobe, inserted sideways, and twisted 90°, and the station said no gag.

Do this: bag an adult who is not breathing. You did it right if you said “one breath every six seconds,” squeezed about 1 second, and stopped when the chest rose.

Do this: put 15 L on the BVM when class style is allowed. You did it right if you still followed that day’s sheet if it wants room air first, then O2.

Do this: name the field impression on medical. You did it right if you said the problem out loud before you finished.
