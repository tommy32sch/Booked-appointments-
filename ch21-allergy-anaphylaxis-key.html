Allergy and anaphylaxis — key

Skin only (itch, hives, rash) is a mild allergic reaction. Do not give epinephrine (Epi). Skin plus a breathing problem, or skin plus low blood pressure, is anaphylaxis. Give Epi. Adult low pressure here is systolic blood pressure (SBP) under 90. The book also counts nausea or vomiting as a second system. Breathing trouble or low pressure is the usual pair with skin. Either breathing trouble or low pressure is enough. You do not need both.

Pruritus is itching. Urticaria is hives (many raised itchy bumps). A wheal is one single bump, like a mosquito bite. Edema is swelling from fluid leaking into tissue. Angioedema is swelling in one area. Tongue and face are common. Dyspnea is hard to breathe. Stridor is a high whistle, usually on inhale. That is the upper airway. You hear it without a stethoscope. Wheezing is a high whistle, usually on exhale. That is the lower airway. You hear it with a stethoscope. Hypersensitivity means allergy. A sympathomimetic is a drug that copies fight-or-flight. Hives from an allergy are not contagious. If it looks like itching and hives, treat it as an allergy even if they say they have none.

The body must be sensitized first. First exposure usually just teaches the body. A later exposure can cause the big reaction. The body dumps histamine. Histamine causes itching, hives, swelling, wide-open vessels, leaky vessels, squeezed lungs, and low heart output.

Epi hits three receptor groups. Alpha receptors squeeze vessels back down, so blood pressure comes up. Beta-1 speeds and strengthens the heart, so output comes up. Beta-2 opens the lower airways. Epi also stops the body from making more histamine. It does not wipe out histamine already in the blood.

This shock is a pipe problem: distributive shock. Same group as septic and neurogenic. Adult SBP under 90 is decompensated shock. Skin here is often red, flushed, and full of hives. It is not always cool, pale, and sweaty.

Four things for every shock: lay them down (supine), high-flow oxygen, keep warm, rapid transport. Extra for anaphylactic shock: call medical control for Epi.

Hard to breathe only → sit them up (High Fowler). Shock → lay them down. Both problems → shock wins. Lay them down. If breathing gets worse, you can use a bag-valve mask (BVM). If sitting them up drops pressure, you cannot easily fix that.

Airway, breathing, and circulation (ABCs) come first. Give oxygen before Epi. The oxygen device comes from rate, rhythm, and quality right now. Adequate breathing but they need oxygen → non-rebreather (NRB). Respiratory failure (still breathing, but slow or shallow and not enough) → BVM. Respiratory arrest → BVM. Tripod, extra muscles, nasal flaring, and head bobbing only prove they are working hard. Hives and itching are what point you to allergy instead of asthma.

Adult Epi is 0.3 mg intramuscular (IM). Child Epi is 0.15 mg IM. Call medical control to give it and to repeat it. The needle can go through clothes. Bare skin is nicer if you have a second. If you only have an adult pen for a child, call the doctor. Do not guess.

If a stinger is still in, scrape it out with a credit card. Do not use tweezers. Tweezers squeeze more venom in. A honeybee stinger can keep pumping venom for about 20 minutes. Take off rings and watches before swelling turns them into a tourniquet. Wash the area. Ice or a cold pack on a sting site.

More than two-thirds of anaphylaxis deaths happen in the first 30 minutes. Airway swelling and low oxygen kill first. Low pressure is second. Penicillin is the most common drug allergy worldwide. Food often shows in 20–30 minutes because it must be absorbed. Injected drugs and stings can hit right away.

Practice

Do this: sort a hornet sting plus rash only from skin plus wheeze or SBP under 90. You did it right if rash only got no Epi and the second system got Epi.

Do this: name pruritus, urticaria, and a wheal out loud. You did it right if itching, hives, and one bump were the three words.

Do this: sit a dyspnea-only patient High Fowler, then lay a shock patient down. You did it right if both problems went supine.

Do this: pick NRB or BVM from rate, rhythm, and quality only. You did it right if slow shallow breathing got a BVM.

Do this: say oxygen first, then call medical control for Epi. You did it right if Epi did not jump the ABCs.

Do this: name adult 0.3 mg IM and child 0.15 mg IM. You did it right if you also said call medical control to give it and to repeat it.

Do this: scrape a practice stinger with a credit card, not tweezers. You did it right if rings and watches came off next.

Do this: wash the sting and put ice on the site. You did it right if you did not tweeze the stinger.

Do this: fire an auto-injector through clothes on a drill dummy. You did it right if the needle still went IM into a big muscle.
