Head and spine injuries — key

Work from the outside in: scalp → skull → bleeds around the brain → the brain itself → the cord.

Primary brain injury already happened. Fight the secondary stuff: low oxygen, low blood pressure, infection.

Trauma + slow heart rate = only two things: pressure in the head, or a spinal cord injury. Look at the blood pressure to tell them apart. Slow heart + HIGH blood pressure = pressure inside the head (Cushing’s). Slow heart + LOW blood pressure = spinal cord / neurogenic shock.

Cushing’s triad: high wide BP, slow heart, irregular breathing. Example: 200/90 (systolic up, diastolic nearer normal). Regular high blood pressure usually climbs together, like 160/110. Slow heart from pressure on the vagus. Irregular breathing = Cheyne-Stokes or ataxic. Same pattern can come from a tumor, extra CSF, or infection. Do not give nitro to drop that BP. Low volume / bleeding can hide the high-BP clue.

Jaw thrust. No NPA. Wrap the head loose. Do not drop the blood pressure on purpose. Keep oxygen up. Open head wound: dry sterile dressing.

Central nervous system = brain + spinal cord. Cerebrum = thinking, language, senses, personality (right brain runs the left body). Cerebellum = balance and coordination. Brainstem = breathing, blood pressure, pupils, temperature — deepest and most protected.

Meninges outside to inside: dura (tough outer), arachnoid (CSF + bigger vessels), pia (on the brain). CSF is made inside the brain (about 125 mL). It cushions. It can leak out only if the skull is cracked. Foramen magnum = big hole at the base of the skull; late rising pressure shoves the brainstem down that hole (herniation). Vagus nerve (cranial nerve 10) goes to the heart — stimulate it → heart slows.

Skull fractures — the bone itself is not the emergency; the brain under it is. Linear ≈ 80%, crack only, feels stable. Depressed = dent or soft spot; do not keep pushing. Open = better for pressure, worse for infection; dry sterile dressing. Basilar = raccoon eyes, Battle’s sign (mastoid bruise behind the ear), CSF from nose or ear.

Intracranial bleeds: epidural (artery, fast) = lucid interval — down → up → down. Subdural (vein, slow) = delayed; older people and alcoholics. Subarachnoid = sudden worst headache. Intracerebral = in the brain itself (trauma name); same problem as hemorrhagic stroke with a different label. There is no “sub-pia” bleed.

Concussion = CT looks normal; about 90% never pass out. Contusion = CT shows bruised brain. You cannot tell them apart on scene — treat as if the brain is damaged.

Cord: motor signals go down; sensory go up. Complete cut = no feel and no move below. Bones can pinch the cord without cutting it — same numbness and paralysis until the pinch is gone; when pressure comes off, feeling and movement can return. MOI: flexion (bent too far forward), extension (head snapped back), axial load (compressed — land on head or feet, diving), distraction (pulled apart — hanging; different from a “distracting injury”).

Board only with a reason: unconscious, numbness or paralysis, neck or back pain after trauma, or you do not believe them. Walking around after the wreck is a reason NOT to board just because of the crash.

Helmet: leave on if it fits, holds the head still, and you can still do airway and breathing. Take off if full-face blocks airway, head rattles inside, arrest, or you cannot immobilize with it on. Two people. Bring the helmet. Football: face mask off, helmet can stay.

Practice

Do this: open the airway with a jaw thrust; refuse NPA. You did it right if jaw thrust was the trauma airway move and NPA stayed out.

Do this: stop scalp bleeding and wrap the head loosely. You did it right if a tight wrap was refused.

Do this: keep oxygen up after a brain injury. You did it right if one minute of low oxygen making death much more likely was the reason.

Do this: read trauma vitals — slow heart + HIGH BP vs slow heart + LOW BP. You did it right if high BP meant head pressure (Cushing’s) and low BP meant spinal / neurogenic.

Do this: name Cushing’s triad and refuse nitro for that high wide BP. You did it right if high wide BP (e.g. 200/90), slow heart, and irregular breathing were listed, and nitro was refused.

Do this: decide the helmet — leave on if it fits, holds still, and you can do airway; remove if full-face blocks airway, head rattles, arrest, or you cannot immobilize with it. You did it right if two people remove it without bending the neck and the helmet comes with you.

Do this: decide whether to board. You did it right if you boarded for unconscious, numb/paralyzed, neck/back pain after trauma, or you do not trust them — and you did NOT board just because they walked after the wreck.

Do this: open head wound gets a dry sterile dressing. You did it right if germs into the brain was the reason, and you did not crash the BP on purpose.
