# Cardiovascular emergencies and heart attacks — detailed

This talk is backups, clots, and what you do for chest pain. Cardiovascular disease is the leading killer here. Lifestyle and genes both count. CPR in public and AEDs in the open matter because people arrest during an MI.

Memorize the path so you can reverse it. Body into the right atrium through the vena cava, tricuspid, right ventricle, pulmonic valve, lungs. Lungs into the left atrium, mitral valve, left ventricle, aorta, body. Left side receives from the lungs and pumps to the body. Fail the left ventricle and fluid is pushed into the alveoli. Right side receives from the body and pumps to the lungs. Fail the right ventricle and the backup is JVD and dependent edema.

Myocardium has automaticity. SA node is the usual pacemaker at 60 to 100. AV node delays so the atria squeeze first. Ventricular fibrillation is chaotic quivering with no pump. Ventricular tachycardia can have a pulse or not. The AED treats pulseless ventricular tachycardia the same as ventricular fibrillation. A shock stops the chaos so the SA node can try again.

Coronary arteries come off the aorta. The muscle does not feed off the blood in the chambers. Plaque narrows the lumen. A clot on the plaque can finish the block. Ischemia is not enough oxygen. MI is cell death.

Stable angina is the same elephant-on-the-chest feeling, but it follows a known work level and eases with rest, oxygen, or nitro in a few minutes. Unstable is the pattern changing. MI is that pain that will not quit, plus shortness of breath, nausea, and a sweat. Palpation and breathing do not change it. Odd presentations still count.

Aspirin works on the clot, not on how the pain feels. Chew 162 to 324 milligrams if they are not allergic. Nitro opens coronaries. 0.4 milligrams under the tongue. Three doses max, five minutes apart, pressure at least 100 each time, no recent erectile-dysfunction meds, no head injury. Oxygen to 94 to 99 percent. Sit chest-pain patients up. Shock lies them down unless they cannot breathe.

CHF is often an old MI that left a weak pump. Left-sided: crackles, pink froth, they sleep sitting up. Sit them up. CPAP if they are awake, breathing, and at least 90 systolic. Right-sided: JVD and swollen legs or sacrum. Cardiogenic shock is the pump failing now. High-flow oxygen, flat, warm, go.

## Practice
Do this: run OPQRST on a classmate chest pain. You did it right if onset, what eases it, quality, radiation, 0 to 10, and time all got a question.

Do this: name aspirin job in one sentence. You did it right if you said it keeps platelets from sticking, not that it kills the pain.

Do this: refuse nitro on a pressure of 92. You did it right if you said the floor is 100.

Do this: pick a device from breathing quality. You did it right if inadequate rate or depth got a bag-valve mask.

Do this: start CPR then turn the AED on. You did it right if the first AED move was turn it on.

Do this: keep a 30:2 adult ratio with two rescuers. You did it right if you did not switch the adult to 15:2.

Do this: call left-sided CHF from crackles and sleeping upright. You did it right if you sat them up and thought CPAP only at 90 or higher.

Do this: treat cardiogenic shock as shock first. You did it right if they went flat unless they could not breathe.
